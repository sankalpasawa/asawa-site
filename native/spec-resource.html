RESOURCE — functional spec SPEC · Token-budget

STATUS: WORKING DRAFT (2026-06-16). Functional spec for the RESOURCE family (worked axis: Token-budget). Product altitude (the WHAT, not the HOW). Produced by the A→B bootstrap path as bounded Work-Atoms (write → critique → fix), rendered deterministically. Spine: D62 P1 (directional/bounded/structured) + P2 (placement).

RUN reads accumulated spend against the finite pool, the allocation rule reserves or queues each competing atom, step completion records the true cost, RECORD reconciles the balance as pool-start = spend + closing balance, and an exhausted pool gates the next intent at SHAPE with a clean allocation-denied.

1. ABSTRACT

RESOURCE is the family of axes that answer one question about every piece of work: what finite stock does this work spend, and is there enough left? Its single shared trait — the answer to the sorting test — is that each axis names a bounded pool that the work draws down and that must reconcile, not merely accumulate: tokens, money, attention, compute slots, rate-limit headroom, a human's time, an account's standing. RESOURCE reads the running spend against the available pool and fires hardest at RUN (when an atom reserves and consumes) and at RECORD (when the pool balance is reconciled). The reader gets the family that turns 'we ran out mid-run, opaquely' into an explicit, auditable wallet: every atom debits a named stock, every parallel claim is allocated or queued by a stated rule, and every run closes with a balance that adds up. Where SHAPE decides how to cut the work and TIME decides when it runs, RESOURCE decides whether the work can afford to run at all.

2. WHAT IT DOES

For the operator, RESOURCE makes the cost of work visible and governable while it happens, not after it has overspent. When several atoms run in parallel and reach for the same pool, the family allocates the stock by a stated rule and queues the losers rather than letting them all drain the wallet until it silently empties mid-run; an operator watching a long parallel job sees one atom reserve its share, another wait, and the pool draw down to a number they can read, not a surprise. When the run ends, the family reconciles — it shows that what started, plus what came in, equals what was spent plus what remains plus what is still reserved — so the operator can trust the ledger instead of guessing why credits vanished. And when a pool is exhausted before a new intent arrives, RESOURCE refuses the work cleanly at the front with a stated reason, so the failure is a legible 'allocation denied, pool empty' rather than a half-finished run and an opaque out-of-credits error.

3. WHY THIS IS ITS OWN FAMILY

RESOURCE exists because scarcity is a distinct concern from shaping, timing, or gating: a pool can be empty even when the plan is perfect, the schedule is met, and authority has said yes. The problem it solves — for any operator running parallel or repeated work against a metered stock — is that without an explicit finite pool the system has no way to say 'stop, there isn't enough', and overspend shows up only as a downstream failure with no ledger to read. It is its own closed family, not folded into a neighbor, because the sorting test isolates exactly one yes: it is the only family whose axes name a finite stock the work spends and must balance. That single-yes test is what keeps the family count principled rather than borrowed from an external tally — each family slot is the closed skeleton that holds exactly one sorting answer, and the answer here ('a finite stock the work spends and reconciles') is held by no other family. So 'cost' does not leak into TIME (which is about when, not how much is left) or into GOVERN (which decides whether an action is allowed, not whether the stock can fund it); it stays its own slot because its sorting answer is its own.

4. PLACE IN THE LOOP

On the shared loop diagram — the same map used on every family page, with only the highlight moving — RESOURCE reads a finite stock and fires across the middle of the loop: it allocates at SHAPE, reserves and dispatches at RUN, and reconciles at RECORD, with EVIDENCE auditing the spend and GROW reading spend trends to reshape the next run. This is faithful to its master-table row, which reads 'a finite stock · spent at RUN · reconciled at RECORD'; the fuller attach spread — SHAPE allocate, RUN reserve/dispatch, RECORD reconcile, EVIDENCE audit-spend, GROW spend-trends — is the same row seen at finer grain. GOVERN wraps these transitions as it wraps all others — authority can still ask or deny on a given spend — but the resource decision itself is a question of stock, not permission: the allocation rule decides who runs now and who waits, and the reconciliation step is where the loop proves the spend was honest. When a pool is exhausted, the consequence travels back to the front, gating the next intent at SHAPE with a clean denial.

5. THE AXES

The family is a small open catalog of finite-stock axes, each carrying its own coordinates — what stock it names, when it fires, and the value-ladder it moves a unit of work through. The load-bearing member is Token-budget (the model-call wallet); siblings include money/$-budget, attention/human-time, compute-slots, and rate-limit headroom — the non-Token-budget rows here are illustrative/minted examples, not a ratified roster. Each axis shares one ladder of states the stock moves through: available → allocated → reserved → spent → reconciled — a categorical progression you sort a unit into, while the underlying quantity (how much remains) is the numeric measure you aggregate across atoms. Most RESOURCE axes are minted on demand rather than built in: an operator names a new pool when a new scarce stock appears, and it joins the catalog if it passes the retention test (it changes who-runs or what-gets-built) and fits the finite-stock sorting answer. The catalog is open; the family slot that holds it is closed.

6. ONE AXIS, WALKED

Token-budget is the axis to walk because it is the one most operators feel first, and one concrete run shows its value changing across the loop. Take a pool of fifteen units shared by two parallel atoms, A and B, each wanting ten. At SHAPE, the allocation rule grants atom A a reservation of ten and queues atom B, because granting both would need twenty against a pool of fifteen. At RUN, A executes and its true spend lands at 9.8, leaving 5.2 in the pool; B — when its turn comes — re-checks the pool, finds its needed 10 is not covered by the 5.2 that remains, and is DENIED (it spends 0) rather than allowed to overdraw. At RECORD, the family reconciles as a two-term identity: pool-start 15 = A's spend 9.8 + closing balance 5.2, with B contributing zero because it was denied. The numbers add up; any gap between 15 and 9.8 + 5.2 would be logged as drift. At EVIDENCE the spend is auditable, and GROW reads the trend so the next SHAPE allocates smarter. The takeaway: the same pool number moves cleanly through reserve, deny, spend, and reconcile, and at every stage it is a number an operator can read, not an opaque drain.

7. BOUNDARIES

RESOURCE owns the finite stock and its balance — it does not own the relationship, the clock, or the verdict, and each adjacent concern belongs to a named sibling. A near-miss that looks like RESOURCE but isn't: 'two atoms contend for one stock' feels like a pool question, but when the question is about the typed link between those two atoms — who-hands-to-whom, who-shares-with-whom — it flips to EDGE, whose shared-resource axis is the very same scarcity seen from between the atoms (the two are one thing viewed two ways, and both pages must point at each other). A second near-miss: 'when does the stock refill / contend over time' flips to TIME, because scheduling contention is just RESOURCE seen over time — the dimension is when, not how-much-is-left. A third: 'may this spend happen at all' flips to GOVERN, which gates the transition auto/ask/deny; RESOURCE answers can-it-afford-it, GOVERN answers is-it-allowed. The sorting-test reason in each case is the single yes: only RESOURCE names a finite stock the work spends and reconciles.

8. WHY THIS GROUPING

These axes group under one family because they share the closed-skeleton/open-flesh rule applied through one sorting answer: every token, dollar, slot, or attention-pool is the same primitive — a finite stock the work spends — so they belong together even though their units differ. The rejected alternative was to scatter them: put 'cost' under TIME (rejected because timing and affordability are independent — you can be on time and broke), or under GOVERN (rejected because a gate decides permission, not sufficiency), or to split every stock type into its own family (rejected because that explodes the closed family skeleton for no new sorting question — a dollar-pool and a token-pool ask the identical question, so they share one slot). Grouping by the finite-stock test keeps the family honest: anything that names a spendable, reconcilable pool is in; anything that doesn't is handed to the sibling the sorting test assigns.

9. PATH TO PRODUCTIZATION

A new RESOURCE axis grows along the same reusable path every axis follows — name-and-define, show, thread, cross-link, ratify — specialized to this family. Name-and-define anchors the new pool against the finite-stock sorting answer; show states its need, advantage, ladder, and a worked contention example; thread places it on the owning company-decomposition surface beside its RESOURCE siblings; cross-link wires it into the loop at SHAPE/RUN/RECORD and names its couplings — to the shared-resource EDGE on one side and to the scaling axis on the other — on both sides, not just one; ratify elevates it from a minted candidate to a first-class axis with a named reconciliation invariant. The detail of that path lives in the axis-productization material and is not re-explained here; what matters at this altitude is that the family's open set has a known door, so a newly scarce stock becomes a governed pool by walking it. This is the family applying the loop's own GROW stage to itself.
