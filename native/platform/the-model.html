Level 1 · Particles — goal, constraints, context, HOW

The four parts every unit is made of, at every level. None can run alone. The fourth particle is named HOW — it is the how of this unit (skill, LLM call, or code); the HOW methodology above is the how of choosing hows. Attempts are logged data ON the HOW particle — a counter and history for systemic purposes, not an object.

The HOW particle carries the attempt log — a field, never a box of its own.

Level 2 · Work-Atom — the smallest unit

RuleStatement
Smallest unitthe how undecided defaults to the intelligence floor — hand it to the LLM; 1..n internal steps allowed under ONE verify
Atomicityatomic in ACCOUNTABILITY: one goal, one verify
Attempt loggingeach run increments the attempt log on HOW (systemic record; retry budget reads it)
No naked work (X1)every atom belongs to a workflow — a single atom is a workflow of one

Level 3 · Workflow (written) → Engine (grown)

A Workflow is the written blueprint: 1..n atoms composed. Run it systemically and it becomes an Engine — the workflow plus everything it has accumulated. No stateless execution (X2): every run happens inside an engine, born at first run with an empty record slice.

Level 4 · Assembly (written) → System (grown) — and the charter is its property

An Assembly is the written composition: which workflows, which glue, which edges. What runs and grows is a System — engines + glue engines operating together, daily, for a goal. The charter is a PROPERTY of the system — its goals, constraints, and authority; one-directional (only systems have charters); pre-provisioned at birth, empty is fine. The standing systems ARE the domain tree; systems cascade, and a nested system is one engine to its parent (X3).

Level 5 · The five functions — Identity, Adaptation, Priority, Coordination, Audit

Ruled 2026-09-20. On the department screen the five are functions, one line each in the list column, each with an output and a chat. Identity is the head: it holds the goal, the done line, the rules (go, ask, refuse, always), the budget and the owner, judges every effect, and is the only function the human speaks with. The word charter is not used on screens. Detail: the five functions · the life cycle · the screen. The paragraph below is the earlier wording, kept for its reasoning.

The five glue types are five engines, and together they form a system of their own — the internal system, present (pre-provisioned, mostly empty at birth) inside every system. The charter states what must be true; the internal system is the machinery that KEEPS it true. Its engines hold the composition state. The maturation ladder is "which internal-system slots have filled yet." The regress stops here: the internal system's engines are crystallized invariants — their verifies ARE their governance.

Running proof in this repo: dispatch/routing policy = priority · marker machinery = coordination · daily governance audit = audit · triage cron = adaptation · permission gates = identity.

Level 6 · Shadow Systems — structured overlays for cross-system work

When work must cross standing systems in a way their composition does not define: do not change the standing systems. Create a shadow system — a structured overlay with its own glue engine that coordinates the standing systems and leaves them untouched. Standing systems may grow discrete and messy; shadow systems are purpose-built and structured. Mandates ("cut cost 15%") and cross-cutting paths (an investor update touching two departments) are shadow systems. Cross-cutting rules are simply the shadow system's charter.

The whole ladder in one line

You write Work-Atoms into Workflows into Assemblies; what runs and grows are Engines and Systems. Two columns (written, grown). Six nouns. Four particles. Charter, domain, tenancy = properties of systems (tenancy design deferred by ruling). Every system carries its internal system; cross-system work gets a shadow system.

What it is, in one paragraph

Native is a feedback loop for work: it turns an intent into done work, writes down what happened, and reads its own record to do better next time. The record is the system's model of itself (the Good Regulator Theorem: to regulate something well you must model it — the record is that model). Everything below is detail inside that one loop.

1 · The two primitives

Build these two well; everything else is them, composed — these are the two product primitives this narrative foregrounds; canon carries 10 typed primitives in total (see §8).

2 · The flow (the loop)

An intent enters → SHAPE turns it into a plan of atoms → they RUN and leave a RECORD → GROW reads the record and improves things → the improvement reshapes the next run. Authority wraps every step.

3 · The axes — one primitive, sorted into families

An axis is a dimension you read a piece of work against. Axes are not a fixed list — they are minted on demand from one primitive (value ↔ axis, ADR-027). What is fixed is the primitive + the families (the slots an axis falls into), sorted by one read-test.

Families = closed skeleton; axes = open, minted flesh. Solid arrows = the original three families (unit-internal); dashed = the three this session surfaced (between-atoms, stock, time).

The three EDGE kinds

Shared-resource edges + Scheduling contention are the same thing — the S2 (coordination) / S3 (resource) rungs of the maturation ladder. TIME dynamics guardrail (canon pitfalls #2 / #12): respond to out-of-control signals only — don't "tamper" against normal variance.

5 · The axes are open — minting vs reworking

Mirrors the two primitives: the Work-Atom is the one doing primitive (everything is it, composed); value↔axis is the one thinking primitive (every axis is it, minted). Users mint axes; only the core author reworks the families.

7 · Honest scope

  • Productization, not implementation. Schemas, thresholds, solvers, simulators are the implementation layer — deferred.
  • TIME is included as axes (the insight), not engines. No scheduler, no system-dynamics simulator in v1 — the critical-path idea and the oscillation warning, yes.
  • The loop models the work graph + its evolution — NOT on-time or stability guarantees. A fully-verified atom set can still miss a deadline or oscillate.

8 · Corrections kept honest

  • Native does NOT lack composition/coordination. Canon already has 5 composition laws + the VSM metasystem; the website pages just under-surface it — this doc surfaces it.
  • Native has more than one canon primitive. Work-Atom is the hero of this narrative; canon lists 10 typed primitives (Domain, Charter, Workflow, Step, Trigger, ExecutionResult, EngineEvent, Tenant, DecisionProvenance, Approval).
