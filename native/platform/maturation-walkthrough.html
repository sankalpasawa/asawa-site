The whole idea in one picture

Imagine a small digital lending company run by Native. It starts as a handful of workflows — little jobs the system does: disburse a loan (W-disburse-loan), verify a borrower's KYC (W-kyc-verify), reconcile the day's EMI collections (W-emi-reconcile), answer a borrower email, handle a payment dispute. That is the bottom rung — lots of jobs, no system holding them together yet.

As the loan book grows, the jobs start to step on each other. Each kind of trouble is a signal that one new part is missing. Native watches the record of what happened, notices the trouble, and proposes one new part — and asks you first. You say yes; the part appears. Do this five times and the pile of jobs has quietly become a system.

The same five-step loop runs for every phase below. Only the trouble it spots — and the part it proposes — changes.

How to read each phase: the orange In the company box is the real moment that triggers it; the diagram shows BEFORE (the trouble) and AFTER (the new part); the green box says how it helps; the blue box points the implementing agent at the spec's detector row (the canonical signal + threshold + landing block — kept in ONE place so the two pages cannot drift).

Phase 1 · Coordination — stop two jobs fighting

In the company: The W-disburse-loan job and a "festival loan offer" campaign job both draw on the same day's disbursal float (the pool of lendable funds). The offer approves fresh loans out of float the disbursal job had already earmarked for sanctioned borrowers. Two jobs, each fine alone, collide.
How it helps: the two jobs stop fighting. The offer now checks the disbursal plan before it commits, so the company never promises funds it has already earmarked. Nothing was rewritten — one small traffic-cop was added between them.
To build it (agent): the spec is canonical — implement the S2 coordination detector row (signal, threshold, and landing block live there; this page adds no build claims). VSM S2 · signal: concrete.

Phase 2 · Priority — decide what gets attention first

In the company: Monday 9am. A failed-disbursal complaint (an angry borrower), three borrower emails, the EMI-reminder batch, and a KYC batch all want to run at once — but there is one daemon and one assistant. The complaint waits behind a blast of routine reminders.
How it helps: the important thing happens first. The angry borrower's complaint jumps the reminder blast, and nothing quietly starves while something routine hogs the assistant.
To build it (agent): the spec is canonical — implement the S3 resource detector row (signal, threshold, and landing block live there). VSM S3 · signal: concrete.

Phase 3 · Audit — catch the silent failure

In the company: Every night the W-emi-reconcile job runs and writes its result to the record. But nobody ever reads that result. One night collections land ₹2,00,000 short of the ledger — and no one notices for a week.
How it helps: silent failures get caught the same night, not a week later. The founder can trust the loan book because something is actually checking it, not just recording it.
To build it (agent): the spec is canonical — implement the S3* audit detector row (signal, threshold, and landing block live there). VSM S3* · signal: softer — needs a definition pass first.

Phase 4 · Adaptation — notice the world moved

In the company: The W-kyc-verify job was written when the credit bureau answered in two minutes and the company could promise same-day disbursal. The bureau now takes a day and mismatch rates have doubled. The job keeps promising on the old assumptions — it is quietly solving last quarter's problem.
How it helps: the system notices it has gone stale and proposes the fix — "your disbursal promise assumes the old bureau turnaround; update it?" — instead of silently breaking promises until you catch it by hand. This is the rung your research calls the "missing 2nd-order loop."
To build it (agent): the spec is canonical — implement the S4 adaptation detector row (signal, threshold, and landing block live there; the only genuinely new home). VSM S4 · signal: softer — needs a definition pass first.

Phase 5 · Identity — stay true to what the company is

In the company: Native, optimizing hard, starts proposing jobs like "approve thinner credit files" and "push short-cycle payday-style loans." Each is efficient — and each quietly betrays the company's whole reason to exist: it is a responsible prime lender.
How it helps: growth stays true to what the company is. The thin-file job gets rejected against the lender's charter; the system can get more efficient without becoming a different, worse business. This is the highest rung — the company's identity now governs everything below it.
To build it (agent): the spec is canonical — implement the S5 identity detector row (signal, threshold, and landing block live there). VSM S5 · signal: softest — needs the most definition.

What you end up with

Five small parts, each added only when the company actually needed it, each approved by a human. Together they are the difference between a pile of jobs and a business that runs itself well: jobs that don't fight (1), attention that goes to what matters (2), books that are actually checked (3), a system that keeps up with reality (4), and growth that stays true to what the lender is (5).
