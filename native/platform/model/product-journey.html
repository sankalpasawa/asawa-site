The Product Journey ONE FEATURE · THREE ERAS

One feature — the lending company decides to launch top-up loans — followed from idea to shipped, three times in its life: the very first journey, the journey once the path has hardened, and the mature ecosystem years in. Same map every time; watch what changes.

How to read every picture: columns are the permanent departments · the tinted band is the feature’s journey crossing them · the pill tokens are what gets handed across each boundary · a padlock means that step has frozen into code.

1 · The map: four permanent systems, one journey crossing them

Strategy, Product, Design and Engineering are standing systems — each with its own rule-book, its own working memory, its own craft. A feature is not a department: it is a journey that crosses all four, picking up a transformed artifact at each boundary — a strategy brief becomes a PRD becomes a design spec becomes an HLD, then an LLD, then a live feature. At each handoff sits a small decision gate: proceed, rework, or stop.

2 · Era one: the first journey ever — slow, hot, and worth it

3 · Era two: the path hardens — same map, gaining padlocks

Canonical owner of the freeze/unfreeze dial rules: Workflow + Engine (story retold at A Department’s Life §5) — this era keeps only the per-run timeline (6 weeks → 2 weeks → days).

4 · Era three: the evolved ecosystem — many journeys, few hands, right seats

This is the mature picture. The departments hum on crystallized machinery. Multiple features flow concurrently through paths that hardened long ago. And the people? They didn’t disappear — they moved to the seats that are deliberately never frozen: the strategy call, taste, and final accountability. Not leftover manual work — chosen seats, where human judgment is the point. Everything below those seats got cheaper and faster on its own, run by run, padlock by padlock.

5 · Inside one department (era three, zoomed) — many journeys, one map

Zoom into Product in the mature company. Three features are alive at once. Watch how one department reads the rest of the company to make its calls — and where its people sit.

Three things to point at when explaining this picture. The lines: each live feature is a colored line using only the stations it needs — top-up v3 rides the fast frozen path; the co-lending pilot stops at a diamond, an honest “a judgment hasn’t been made yet.” The dashed evidence line: Product does not decide its roadmap from opinion — it reads what Design, Engineering and Collections actually recorded (what got cut, what overran, what borrowers did), and its decisions flow back down as conditions those departments work inside. Future strategy is decided from downstream past — that loop is the dashed line. The faces: two stations are held by people, on purpose. The “pricing check” and “compliance look” stations drawn here are defined as full engine cards (E-price-loan, E-comply-check) at Inside the Product Vertical — stations defined there, story drawn here.

What stays human, department by department

DepartmentThe calls left to its peopleThe shared pattern
StrategyRisk appetite · which market to enter · where capital goesEvery human lever is a tradeoff threshold: the machine brings the evidence and the options; the person sets where the line sits — and owns it
ProductWhat to build next · scope cuts under deadline · the risk-appetite line / pricing thresholds (the E-price-loan human seat — the engine itself runs 3-of-5 atoms frozen)
DesignTaste · brand voice · the simplicity call (what to leave OUT)
EngineeringArchitecture bets · build vs buy · how much tech debt to carry
PROPOSED The depiction convention (minted on this page only — no decision-sheet row yet; use as this page’s legend, not standing canon, until routed and ruled): stations = a system’s engines · colored lines = journeys threading their stations · padlock = frozen · circled face = a human seat · orange diamond = an unresolved judgment · dashed gray = the evidence line. A map drawn this way stays honest: it shows the machine’s speed AND the places still waiting on a person.
The whole page in one line: departments are permanent, journeys borrow them, the path hardens with every trip, evidence flows up while decisions bind down — and people end up exactly where judgment matters most.
