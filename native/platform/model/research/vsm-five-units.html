VSM: the five units THE VOCABULARY · research

Where Native's five functions of a department come from. Stafford Beer's Viable System Model says that any organisation which keeps itself going has the same five jobs inside it, and that the same five appear again inside every team of it. Native's five functions — Identity, Adaptation, Priority, Coordination, Audit — map onto Beer's five units almost one to one. This page carries the research; the ruled page is The five functions.

The five units, one by one

Beer built the model from operations research and cybernetics (Ashby's law of requisite variety), on the pattern of the human nervous system, and wrote it up in Brain of the Firm (1972), The Heart of Enterprise (1979) and Diagnosing the System for Organizations (1985). It was tried once at national scale (Project Cybersyn, Chile, 1971-73). No standards body owns it; the Metaphorum community keeps it.

Decision-grade takeaway (from the research). Native's five functions are independently validated by VSM (theory), Scrum's five events (practice) and the Incident Command System (crisis practice): three unrelated traditions converged on the same five jobs. What none of them give us, and the research says we must add: (1) the wire between Priority and Adaptation (Beer's S3-S4 homeostat); (2) severity-scaled activation instead of always-on glue (ICS); (3) a metered charter rule with a named author, reviewer, approver and arbiter (SRE error-budget policy); (4) an implementation-rate ledger as Adaptation's own health metric (Toyota kaizen). Items 2-4 come from the sibling traditions; the full comparison is in the source file.

The mechanics worth reusing

MechanismWhat it isSource
Variety engineeringEvery channel between units must carry more variety than it transmits — Ashby's law applied as a channel-capacity design constraintWikipedia: VSM
Algedonic channelA standing pain/pleasure alert wired through every level: when actual performance falls outside capability an alarm escalates — but only after a defined per-level time window for self-correction has expiredcompiled research (VSM + Cybersyn)
Performance indicesContinuous comparison of actual performance against capability per unit — monitoring as homeostasis, never a per-task done-check (Beer's actuality / capability / potentiality triple; moderate confidence)businessballs
RecursionThe identical S1-S5 template inside every viable unit at every depth ("cybernetic isomorphism")Metaphorum
POSIWID"The purpose of a system is what it does" — read purpose from observed behaviour, never from the written charterWikipedia: POSIWID
  • Verification model. No unit-level done-check anywhere; verification is continuous homeostasis (indices in bounds) plus S3* spot audit.
  • Exception model. The algedonic signal with a time-boxed autonomy window.
  • Learning loop. S4 plus the S3-S4 homeostat.
  • Authority model. S5 at each level; the autonomy rule: "maximise the freedom of participants, within all practical constraints."

The one lived instance — Project Cybersyn (Chile, 1971-73)

Daily production indicators per plant (paper to telex; about 500 teleprinters claimed, disputed) fed Cyberstride, which detected statistical exceptions, which escalated through the algedonic time-box to the operations room. A factory joined the system only once it was modelled and transmitting — literally "an engine is born at first run". The fatal limit: hand-modelling each factory was the bottleneck (about 20 factories fully modelled in two years). Proof of the glue's value: during the October 1972 truck strike the shared real-time record coordinated supply with roughly 200 trucks. Sources: Wikipedia: Project Cybersyn, 99% Invisible.

In a lending company's words. S2 is the shared batch calendar that stops the loan-disbursal engine and the EMI-reconciliation engine colliding on the same core-banking API window. S3* is the audit function re-verifying, unannounced, a sample of closed KYC records directly against the registry, bypassing the KYC engine's own reports. The algedonic signal is a KYC-vendor outage that the KYC engine fails to self-correct within its window and which escalates straight to the department.

Where Beer argues against our design

#Beer's evidenceWhat it argues against in Native
1Audit is a sporadic channel (S3*), not a standing system. The metasystem exists to serve S1 with "maximise the freedom of participants"; S3 intruding into S1 is a named pathology (Wikipedia: VSM)Native promoted Audit to a full always-on function. VSM says continuous audit becomes intrusive control; the sporadic-bypass design is deliberate, not primitive
2Recursion is total in the model, but Cybersyn could only staff about 20 modelled factories in two years, and "unoccupied recursion levels" is a known VSM diagnosisMandating all five functions at every depth from birth risks five template functions that exist but never live — provisioning theatre
3POSIWID: the purpose of a system is what it does, not what its charter saysA charter record read as the department's identity is only as true as the record of what the department actually did; Audit has to read behaviour, not the charter

Open calls (founder)

  • Is Audit a standing function (our ruling) or an S3*-style sporadic channel owned by Priority (Beer's design)?
  • Does the department's assembly mandate the Priority-Adaptation wire (S3-S4 homeostat), and what is its interchange record?
  • Which functions are always on at a department's birth and which activate on load? Candidate: Identity + Audit always on; Coordination / Priority / Adaptation activate on signals.
  • Index vocabulary for Priority's reporting: adopt Beer's actuality / capability / potentiality triple, or reuse the run-count / variance / cost fields already in the record?
