EDGE — functional spec SPEC · handoff (cross-boundary handoff variant)

STATUS: WORKING DRAFT (2026-06-16). Functional spec for the EDGE family (worked axis: handoff (cross-boundary handoff variant)). Product altitude (the WHAT, not the HOW). Produced by the A→B bootstrap path as bounded Work-Atoms (write → critique → fix), rendered deterministically. Spine: D62 P1 (directional/bounded/structured) + P2 (placement).

An EDGE is wired at SHAPE, checked and exercised as the work runs (a handoff fires on producer-complete — its cross-boundary form checked at the crossing before the cross-unit call, a shared-resource edge arbitrates at contention), logged at RECORD, and read by GROW over time (the GROW Cross-unit axis driving the cross-unit grant) to keep, tighten, or drop the link — with GOVERN wrapping every crossing and the default being isolation until a link is deliberately wired.

1. ABSTRACT

*EDGE is the family of axes you read against the link between two work-atoms — never against either atom alone.* Where the other five families read a single unit (its input, its record, its gate, its stock, its clock), EDGE reads the relationship: the handoff between them, the one finite thing they both want, or the shared whole they each build a piece of. Its answer to the sorting test is the only YES on "does it read the relationship between two atoms?" — that single question is what makes it its own slot. EDGE is wired at SHAPE (the connection is planned before the work runs) and grown at GROW (a link's usage is read over time and the link kept, tightened, or dropped). The reader gets the part of the model that decomposition alone leaves blank: once you cut work into atoms, EDGE is what governs how those atoms touch — so handoffs are ordered, contention is arbitrated, and coherence is held across pieces built apart.

2. WHAT IT DOES

EDGE makes the connections between pieces of work first-class, so an operator can see and govern them instead of discovering them as breakage. When work fans out into atoms that must touch, EDGE is what orders the handoff so a consumer never reads a half-finished output, arbitrates the moment two atoms reach for the same finite pool so one proceeds and one waits with a reason, and holds a shared standard so independently built pieces still cohere into one whole. The operator names who may hand to whom and what they share — and the system enforces those links, watches how they are actually used, and surfaces the ones gone stale for tightening. When a handoff crosses an isolation boundary — one unit handing to a sibling unit — the same link is treated as explicit and logged rather than ambient: the cross-boundary form is still a handoff, governed by GOVERN at the crossing and grown by the GROW Cross-unit axis over time. The default is isolation: nothing crosses a boundary until an EDGE is deliberately wired, so the blast radius of any one atom stays bounded and every connection has a named owner and an audit trail.

3. WHY THIS IS ITS OWN FAMILY

Decomposition creates a blind spot: the moment you cut one job into many atoms, the connections between them become load-bearing — and no single-atom axis can read a connection, because a relationship is not a property of either endpoint. EDGE solves this for the operator who has fanned work out and now needs the seams to hold: ordering, contention, and coherence. It is a closed family of its own, not folded into GOVERN, because GOVERN gates every transition of one unit while EDGE reads the standing link between two — a different reader, a different lifecycle (wired at SHAPE, grown at GROW, not evaluated at every gate). It is not folded into RESOURCE either: a shared-resource edge is about the relationship of contention, while RESOURCE is the finite stock itself. The six-family count stays principled and inside the proven 4-6 structuring range; EDGE earns its slot because the relationship is the one thing the other five structurally cannot see.

4. PLACE IN THE LOOP

On the shared loop diagram — the same INTENT to SHAPE to RUN to RECORD to EVIDENCE to GROW map used on every page, with GOVERN wrapping every transition — EDGE lights up at SHAPE and at GROW, the two ends of a link's life. It reads the link between atoms; it is wired at SHAPE (the connection is planned and authority-checked before the work runs) and grown at GROW (accumulated usage of the link is read and the link kept, tightened, or dropped for the next run). In between, an EDGE acts as the work runs — a handoff fires when its producer completes, a shared-resource edge arbitrates at the moment of contention — and every such crossing is logged at RECORD so EVIDENCE can audit it and GROW can act on the trend. GOVERN still wraps each crossing: an EDGE says a link may exist, never that a given transition across it bypasses authority.

5. THE AXES

EDGE holds exactly three relationship axes, each carrying its own coordinates — what it reads, when it fires, and the states it moves through. Handoff reads one atom's output as another's input; it fires at RUN when the producer completes and needs an ordering plus a usable output, moving an atom from waiting to fed to consumed. Shared-resource reads two atoms' competing claim on one finite pool; it fires at the moment of contention and needs an allocation rule (first-come, priority, or budget-order), moving claimants through available to allocated to one-waits — this axis is the categorical seam that the RESOURCE family measures numerically on the other side. Must-cohere reads two atoms that each build a piece of one whole; it fires across the whole span of parallel work and needs a standard agreed up front or an integration step at the end, moving from divergent to checked to coherent. All three are categorical relationships you sort and enforce; shared-resource is additionally the categorical face of a claim whose magnitude RESOURCE aggregates. One important variant lives inside handoff rather than beside it: a cross-unit delegation is a cross-boundary handoff — a handoff that crosses a Tenant boundary, sharing the waiting/fed/consumed ladder but carrying a standing grant whose isolate -> coordinate -> delegate lifecycle is driven by the GROW Cross-unit axis, not by a fourth EDGE axis.

6. ONE AXIS, WALKED

Take handoff, the flagship EDGE axis — the simplest typed link — and walk one instance through the loop in its load-bearing cross-boundary form, so the trace also shows what a cross-unit delegation actually is. The takeaway first: a handoff orders a producer before its consumer, and when it crosses a unit boundary it is wired explicitly, leaves a trail when it fires, and is judged by its usage over time — so reach is never ambient and never permanent. At SHAPE, branch A is wired with a cross-boundary handoff to a specific approval step on sibling branch B; with no link wired, the call would hit the default boundary stop and fail closed (state: isolated). The work runs: A's producer completes, the wired handoff is authority-checked at the crossing, and the output moves across — B's approval step goes from waiting to fed, then to consumed when it reads A's output (state at the seam: coordinated, then delegated). RECORD logs that the cross-boundary handoff fired, by whom, against what, when. EVIDENCE can answer "why did this cross-unit call succeed?" from that trail. Then the GROW Cross-unit axis reads the accumulated usage: ninety days of activity says the link is still earning its place, so it is kept; had usage gone idle, GROW would have flagged it for tightening, after which future calls fall back to the boundary stop. The link's whole life — isolated, coordinated, delegated/consumed, judged, kept-or-tightened — is the cross-unit grant lifecycle, owned by GROW Cross-unit (the handoff's own ladder is waiting -> fed -> consumed); shown here in one trace.

7. BOUNDARIES — WHAT THIS FAMILY IS NOT

EDGE owns the link between atoms and nothing else; each adjacent concern flips to the sibling the sorting test assigns. A near-miss that looks like EDGE but is not: "may this action proceed, auto/ask/deny?" looks like governing a crossing, but it gates a single transition of one unit, so it belongs to GOVERN — the sorting flag is that it reads a gate, not a relationship. "How much of the pool is left and did it reconcile?" looks like the shared-resource edge, but it reads a finite stock's magnitude, so it belongs to RESOURCE — EDGE owns the categorical fact of contention between two atoms, RESOURCE owns the quantity; this is one coupling seen from both sides, the S2/S3 rungs of the maturation ladder, and both docs name it. "When must this run and does it clash on the schedule?" looks like coordinating two atoms in time, but scheduling contention is RESOURCE seen over time, owned jointly by RESOURCE and TIME, not by EDGE. "Should a cross-unit grant be kept, tightened, or revoked as it scales?" reads the link's usage over time, so the grant lifecycle itself belongs to the GROW Cross-unit axis — EDGE supplies the cross-boundary handoff that GROW then grows. For the gating of crossings see the GOVERN family; for the finite pool behind a shared-resource edge see the RESOURCE family; for clash-over-time see the TIME family; for the cross-unit grant lifecycle see the GROW family.

8. WHY THIS GROUPING

These three axes group as one family because they share a single reader — the relationship between two atoms — and that shared reader is exactly the sorting test's EDGE question; the grouping rule is the sorting test, not surface similarity. The rejected alternative was to scatter them: fold shared-resource into RESOURCE and let cross-unit handoffs live in GOVERN. That was rejected because it would split one reader (the link) across two families, leaving handoff and must-cohere homeless and forcing GOVERN to carry standing links it does not gate per-transition. A second tempting move — promoting cross-unit delegation to a co-equal fourth EDGE axis — is also rejected: delegation is the cross-boundary form of the handoff axis, grown by the GROW Cross-unit axis, so minting it as a fourth axis would double-count one link and break the catalog's three-axis count. The closed-skeleton / open-flesh principle settles both: the family slot is closed at "reads the relationship" and the catalog at three axes, while new relationship variants (a coherence-contract variant, a new grant scope) mint into an existing axis as flesh rather than spawning a fourth axis or a new family — and a genuinely new closed-skeleton axis escalates to the core author rather than silently bending EDGE's boundary.

9. PATH TO PRODUCTIZATION

A new EDGE variant has a known path, which is what makes the family's open-flesh-on-closed-skeleton claim real: name and define it against the sorting test (does it read the relationship between two atoms?), confirm it slots into one of the three existing axes rather than claiming a fourth, show its need / advantage / how / example as an operator feature, thread it onto the owning page beside its sibling EDGE axes, cross-link it into the loop at SHAPE and GROW and to its named neighbors, then ratify it. What ratifies an EDGE variant is the invariant that a link never bypasses the stringency it crosses — a cross-boundary handoff is a ceiling, not a floor — together with a defined grown-over-time rule (for cross-unit handoffs, the GROW Cross-unit keep-if-used, tighten-if-idle rule) and the boundary-stop fallback when no link exists. A candidate that fits no existing axis is a closed-skeleton change: it does not mint silently, it escalates to the core author and must be reconciled into the family catalog before any spec asserts it. This is the family's own GROW stage applied to itself: the family that has been defined, placed, cataloged, bounded, and justified now shows how it grows. The generic method lives in the axis-productization material; this section points there rather than re-explaining it, and points down to the owning platform pages where EDGE axes are threaded.

Acceptance criteria (black-box tests)

  • Abstract (section 1) survives standalone: a reader quoting only it learns what EDGE is, its single sorting-test YES (reads the relationship between two atoms), where it fires (wired at SHAPE, grown at GROW), and what the reader gets — with no other section needed
  • Reads-WHAT / fires-WHEN match the master family table verbatim: EDGE reads 'the link between atoms', fires 'wired at SHAPE, grown at GROW' — and no section contradicts that row
  • EDGE is catalogued at EXACTLY three axes (handoff / shared-resource / must-cohere) in section 5, matching axes.html and edges.html; cross-unit delegation appears only as the cross-boundary form of handoff, never as a fourth axis
  • Section 7 names the exact sibling family that owns each adjacent concern (GOVERN for per-transition gating, RESOURCE for the finite stock, TIME for clash-over-time, GROW Cross-unit for the cross-unit grant lifecycle) via convert-flip-flag, with no 'see other families' hand-waving
  • Exactly one worked micro-trace (section 6) walks one handoff across loop stages with named states (isolated -> coordinated -> delegated/consumed -> kept/tightened), not one trace per axis; the cross-unit grant is attributed to the GROW Cross-unit axis
  • Cross-family coupling is named on the EDGE side and attributed to the matching sibling: shared-resource edge = the RESOURCE axis seen between two atoms (S2/S3 maturation rungs); scheduling contention = RESOURCE over time (TIME); cross-unit grant lifecycle = GROW Cross-unit axis
  • Product altitude is held throughout: WHAT and WHY only, no schema, no code, no field-level implementation, and no imperative 'configure X' sentence
  • Tables are reserved for genuinely comparable rows (the axis catalog and the looks-like/belongs-to map); the spine and connective tissue stay prose
