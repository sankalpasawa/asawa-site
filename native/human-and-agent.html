Human and Agent BUSINESS · the long view

By H1, the shift, every person in a company has an agent organisation of their own. The work moves to it in three waves. The person keeps the seat: the want, the stamp, the rule, and the liability.

Why this picture: three waves, in this order, and why the person's box never empties
  • Claim. Work leaves people for their agent orgs in a fixed order, and what never leaves is the seat: the want, the stamp, the rule, the liability.
  • Why wave 1 is paperwork. Search, drafting, booking and billing are the work a machine can check against a closed list. On Sutra that is what engines already do: a site from words in 4 minutes, a web search filed with its sources (Human Simulation runs 3 and 5, 2026-09-29/30).
  • Why wave 2 is the manager. Deciding who does what and when is the work Adaptation and Priority already do in a department: a line of engines shaped from the owner's words, reordered by one sentence. The manager's box empties before the maker's.
  • Why wave 3 is other organisations. One agent org asking another for a filing needs a market: who may call whom, a price, a promise with a time. That has not run yet; it is rung 3 of the experiment.
  • Why the person's box does not empty. Three questions sort every piece of work: can a machine check it, does it need a body, must someone be liable. The want, the taste and the signature fail the first and third, so they stay.
  • What would change it. Law that lets an agent org carry liability (the seat shrinks further), or a run where an engine's work has to be finished by hand (wave 1 is slower than drawn).

Two horizons, not two years. H1, the shift: the point where the fastest industries have moved and the incumbents are mid-change; about five years out as of 2026. H2, the settled state: the curves flat; about fifteen. The years are placeholders; the shocks set them. The evolution frame shows both on the curves.

What this means for a business

1
Headcount stops being the size of the company. Stamps per person per day is. A person who runs five departments on ten minutes each is five businesses, not one job.
2
The manager role goes first, not last. Deciding who does what is the work agent orgs already do; the maker's hands and the owner's taste go last.
3
Payroll turns into a token bill plus a seat. Thinking gets cheap and gets used more; bodies, signatures and trust get dearer.
4
Every industry keeps its need and loses its middle. The finding, drafting, booking and billing between the person who needs and the person who delivers is what disappears.
5
The next wants are about trust, not output. Provenance, curation, trust between agent orgs, fair trade, owning one's own context. Each needs a person to set the rule and an agent org to keep the ledger.
