GOVERN — functional spec SPEC · Compliance-rule

STATUS: WORKING DRAFT (2026-06-16). Functional spec for the GOVERN family (worked axis: Compliance-rule). Product altitude (the WHAT, not the HOW). Produced by the A→B bootstrap path as bounded Work-Atoms (write → critique → fix), rendered deterministically. Spine: D62 P1 (directional/bounded/structured) + P2 (placement).

Before any transition commits — most sharply when a work-atom is about to act on the outside world or write a learned change back — GOVERN classifies the proposed action and returns one of three ordered verdicts on its canonical auto / ask / deny ladder (each axis may surface those rungs under its own synonyms): auto (proceed and report), ask (pause for a named counter-party), or deny (refuse and explain why), then leaves an answerable record of the call.

1. Abstract

GOVERN is the family that answers a single question at every transition in the loop: may this proceed, and on whose authority? Every axis in GOVERN shares one thing — it gates a transition with a three-rung verdict whose canonical form is auto / ask / deny. That is its answer to the sorting test: a SHAPE axis reads the fresh input, a GROW axis accumulates on the unit, a RESOURCE axis is a finite stock — a GOVERN axis decides whether a change is allowed to happen at all. GOVERN does not do the work and does not improve the work; it wraps the work, sitting over INTENT, SHAPE, RUN, RECORD, EVIDENCE, and GROW alike, and biting hardest where an action leaves the system or a learned change writes back. What the operator gets is a system that can act on its own where it is safe to, stops and asks a real person where the stakes warrant it, refuses cleanly where a binding rule forbids it, and can always answer the question why was this allowed, or why was this blocked.

2. What it does

GOVERN gives the operator a system that is trusted to act without becoming a system that acts blindly. As work flows through the loop, every transition that could spend something irreversible, touch the outside world, or rewrite how the system behaves is held up to an authority dimension before it commits, and the operator sees the action proceed automatically when it is routine and reversible, get paused for their decision when it is consequential, or get refused when it crosses a binding line. The operator never has to watch everything to stay in control — they set where the line sits, and the family enforces that line on every transition while it runs, surfacing only the calls that genuinely need a human. And because every verdict is recorded with its reason, the operator can replay any decision after the fact and get a straight answer to who decided this and why, turning trust from a leap of faith into something inspectable.

3. Why this is its own family

GOVERN solves the control problem an autonomous work-loop creates for the operator who is accountable for it: a system that shapes, runs, records, and improves on its own will, without a gate, eventually take an action the operator would never have sanctioned. It is its own closed family — and not folded into SHAPE or GROW — because deciding whether a change may happen is a categorically different act from deciding how to shape the work or learning to do it better; the sorting test isolates it cleanly with one question, gates a transition with an auto / ask / deny verdict. The six-family count stays principled rather than arbitrary: GOVERN is one of six closed slots and it earns a slot because authority is orthogonal to every other family — any axis in any family can be gated, so the gate cannot live inside any one of them.

4. Place in the loop

On the shared loop diagram — the same INTENT -> SHAPE -> RUN -> RECORD -> EVIDENCE -> GROW map used on every page, with only the highlight moving — GOVERN is the dashed wrap drawn around the whole loop, because unlike its siblings it does not occupy a single stage. Its reads-WHAT is every transition and its fires-WHEN is throughout, verbatim from the master family table; the wrap is the visual form of throughout. The highlight that moves for GOVERN is not a box lighting up but the wrap thickening, with two stages marked where it bites hardest: RUN, where a proposed action is about to leave the system for the outside world, and GROW, where a learned change is about to write back and reshape the next run. The reader should leave this section seeing GOVERN not as a step the work passes through but as the membrane every step must pass — the family that turns a loop that can act into a loop that is allowed to act.

5. The axes

The catalog below is GOVERN's open set today — the family is a closed slot, but the axes inside it are minted on demand and grow. Each row carries its own coordinates: what it reads, when it fires, and the verdict ladder it moves a transition through. There is ONE canonical ladder for the whole family — auto -> ask -> deny, three ordered rungs from permissive to refusing — and any other words a row shows are surface synonyms naming those same three rungs, not a different ladder. Authority is the load-bearing, built-in axis; Compliance-rule is the worked example axis this spec follows; the rest are illustrative/minted, showing how the slot fills out. Every GOVERN axis is categorical-to-sort, not numeric-to-aggregate — its output is a verdict (one of the three rungs), not a quantity you sum; you read a GOVERN axis to branch, you do not total it. (Rows, each name | reads | fires | ladder rendered against the canonical auto/ask/deny: Authority | the proposed change + who is asking | every transition | auto / ask / deny (built-in). Compliance-rule | the proposed action against a binding external rule | RUN boundary, before external dispatch | ok=auto / review=ask / blocked=deny — surface synonyms ok/review/blocked, matching the canon axis-productization page (illustrative/minted). Trust-rung | the actor's accumulated standing within a scope | as the gate decides ask-vs-auto | three rungs naming WHERE the auto/ask/deny line sits: deny-floor / ask-by-default / auto-within-scope (illustrative/minted). Reversibility | whether the action can be undone | every transition | auto-eligible / ask-floor / deny-for-truly-irreversible (illustrative/minted).) The ladder is the same shape across the family — three ordered verdicts from permissive to refusing — which is what makes them one family rather than four unrelated checks; every row above renders the SAME three canonical rungs, however it labels them.

6. One axis, walked

Walked once on the load-bearing example, Compliance-rule resolves a single proposed action to one of its three rungs — ok / review / blocked, which are this axis's synonyms for the canonical auto / ask / deny — before it can commit. Take a marketing message about to be sent to a customer segment. As the run reaches the point where the message would leave the system for the outside world, GOVERN does not let it pass unexamined: it classifies the proposed send against the binding rule (does this touch a PII segment without pre-review?). The send is irreversible and the segment is sensitive, so the verdict is not ok — it resolves to review (the ask rung), and the transition pauses for the named counter-party rather than firing. Had the same message gone to a non-PII internal list, the same axis would have resolved to ok (the auto rung) and the send would proceed-and-report; had it violated a hard line outright, blocked (the deny rung), refused with the rule cited. Whatever the verdict, after the decision is made the call is written down with its reason so the operator can later ask why was this blocked and get the rule, not a shrug. One action in, one of three verdicts out, one answerable record left — that is the whole mechanism, and every other GOVERN axis is a variation on it.

7. Boundaries — what this family is NOT

GOVERN owns the verdict on a transition; it does not own the things that transition touches, and each near-miss hands off to a named sibling. A finite stock being spent — tokens, budget, a rate limit — looks like something to govern, but the stock itself and its allocation rule belong to RESOURCE (the sorting flip: a stock you spend is not a verdict you issue; GOVERN may gate the spend, RESOURCE owns the pool — see resource). A typed link deciding who-may-call-whom across an isolation boundary looks like an authority call, and it is governed, but the link itself is an EDGE — specifically the cross-unit handoff variant, the delegation grant — because it reads the relationship between two atoms rather than gating every transition (see edges and authority-tenancy). A check that fires on a schedule or flags a missed cadence looks like a gate, but when it is about over time it is TIME; GOVERN only enters when TIME's diagnostic is consulted at the next gate (see time). And learning that a practice is now safe enough to auto-approve is a GROW act (the trust-rung climbing); GOVERN reads that accumulated standing but does not produce it (see evolution). The rule of thumb: if the axis issues a permit-or-refuse verdict it is GOVERN; if it owns the thing being permitted, it is the sibling.

8. Why this grouping

These axes group under one family because they pass the same sorting test — each gates a transition with the canonical auto / ask / deny verdict — and splitting them would scatter the one decision (may this proceed?) across the system where it could be bypassed. The rejected alternative was to let each family carry its own gate: a spend-gate inside RESOURCE, a write-gate inside GROW, a send-gate inside SHAPE. That was rejected because authority is orthogonal to what is being gated — the same auto/ask/deny logic applies whether the transition spends a token, writes a doctrine, or sends a message — so a per-family gate would duplicate the mechanism four times and leave no single place to set or audit the line. The grouping rule is the closed-skeleton / open-flesh principle: GOVERN is one closed slot, and the axes inside it (Authority, Compliance-rule, Trust-rung, Reversibility) are open flesh minted as new kinds of permission questions arise, all rendering the one canonical three-rung ladder under their own surface labels.

9. Path to productization

A new GOVERN axis has a known path, which is what makes the open-set claim real rather than aspirational: it follows the same name -> show -> thread -> cross-link -> ratify route every axis takes, specialized to this family. A would-be GOVERN axis threads into the authority surface — it sits beside Authority, Trust-rung, and the Approval gate as a sibling dimension — and it earns its place only if it issues a verdict on the canonical auto/ask/deny ladder that the existing dimensions cannot already produce; if it needs a rung or a dimension beyond the current authority model, that is the escalation signal that reshapes the closed skeleton, and only the core author may do that. What ratifies a GOVERN axis is a deliberate, governed act elevating it to first-class authority, never an automatic mint, precisely because a gate that anyone could add on the fly would be a gate that defends nothing. The full generic method lives in axis-productization; the owning surface this family threads into is authority-tenancy — this section points there and stays out of the how.

Acceptance criteria (black-box tests)

  • Every section leads with its takeaway sentence (inverted pyramid) and the abstract survives being quoted standalone — a reader knows in 30 seconds that GOVERN is the family that gates transitions with auto/ask/deny.
  • Exactly ONE canonical verdict ladder (auto/ask/deny) is declared for the whole family, stated once as the family's answer to the sorting test; every section-5 catalog row renders those same three rungs, and any other words (ok/review/blocked, deny-floor/ask-by-default/auto-within-scope, auto-eligible/ask-floor/deny) are explicitly mapped as surface synonyms of the canonical rungs — no row silently uses a different or two-rung ladder.
  • Section 4 places GOVERN on the SAME shared loop as the dashed wrap (reads=every transition, fires=throughout, verbatim from the master family table), biting hardest at RUN and GROW — no contradiction with the GOVERN row in the axes catalog.
  • Section 7 names the EXACT sibling family for each adjacent concern (RESOURCE for the stock, EDGE — the cross-unit handoff/delegation variant — for the cross-unit link, TIME for scheduled/cadence checks, GROW for the trust-rung climb) via convert->flip->flag, and links to resource, edges, authority-tenancy, time, and evolution by name.
  • Exactly one worked micro-trace (section 6) walks the load-bearing example axis (Compliance-rule) through a concrete run resolving to a named verdict (review = the ask rung), with ok (auto) and blocked (deny) shown as contrasting branches and an answerable record left; the trace uses the SAME ladder (ok/review/blocked, mapped to auto/ask/deny) that section 5 assigns to Compliance-rule, and both agree with the canon axis-productization page.
  • The verdict vocabulary is self-consistent across the doc: the abstract, runtime one-liner, section 5 Compliance-rule row, and section 6 trace all name this axis's verdicts identically and all reduce to the one canonical auto/ask/deny ladder.
  • The whole spec stays at product altitude — no schema, no code, no field/primitive-level implementation detail (no field names, no primitive names) appears in any section body.
