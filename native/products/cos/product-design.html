A. Foundations — who, why, what success (§2.F.A carried verbatim)

Before any flow, three answers: who is the operator, what jobs do they hire Native for, what does winning look like at 30 / 60 / 90 days. Everything downstream in §2.F is justified by these three.

A.1 Personas

Native serves four operator archetypes. Same engine, same journeys — different dominant jobs and different tolerance for autonomy.

PersonaEssence (one line)Dominant jobs
P1 — Founder / CEORuns a company, time-starved, decides constantly, hates ceremony.Offload research + drafting + tracking. Capture directions before they evaporate. Surface what needs decision today.
P2 — Ops LeadRuns the day inside a team, owns recurring processes, lives in checklists.Convert one-off asks into standing routines. Watch SLAs. Reduce the "did this get done?" tax.
P3 — Solo CreatorOne-person shop. Builder + marketer + support + bookkeeper.Compress role-switching cost. Pick up dropped threads. Keep a single trustworthy memory of the business.
P4 — COO of T3 ClientSenior operator inside a client co (Testlify, Dharmik) using Native through Sutra.Run cross-functional ops without re-briefing each ask. Get answers their team can act on. Show their CEO traction.

P1 + P3 share intensity-of-context. P2 + P4 share volume-of-recurrence. Native must serve both shapes from the same surface.

A.2 Jobs-to-be-done

The operator hires Native for five jobs. Each is phrased in their voice.

  • JTBD-1 (Task). When I have a concrete thing to get done and don't want to do it myself, I want Native to run it end-to-end so that my hour goes to the next thing, not this one.
  • JTBD-2 (Query). When I need to know something — a fact, a status, a synthesis — I want Native to return a trustworthy answer with its sources so that I can decide without a research detour.
  • JTBD-3 (Directive). When I want how-things-work to change going forward, I want Native to capture the rule once and apply it from then on so that I don't re-explain my preferences every session.
  • JTBD-4 (Feedback). When the output was wrong, off, or could be better, I want Native to absorb the correction and not repeat the miss so that the system gets sharper instead of just busier.
  • JTBD-5 (New Idea). When I have a half-formed thought, I want Native to hold it, sharpen it, and surface it back at the right moment so that ideas stop dying in my notes app.

A.3 North Star — OHS/wk (Operator-Hours Saved per Week)

One number. How many hours of operator time did Native take off the operator's plate this week — by doing work, returning answers, or preventing rework.

OHS/wk is measured per operator, not per company. It counts only work Native completed to operator satisfaction (no rework loop in the next 24h). Hours the operator spent supervising or correcting Native are subtracted.

WindowOHS/wk target bandWhat "good" feels like
Day 303 – 6 hrs/wk"It handles a handful of recurring asks. I check its work."
Day 608 – 12 hrs/wk"It owns several standing routines. I spot-check, don't re-run."
Day 9015 – 20 hrs/wk"It runs a real slice of my day. I notice when it's offline."

Why OHS/wk and not message count, task count, or "engagement". Volume metrics reward Native for being chatty. OHS/wk rewards Native for actually removing work. If Native sends 100 messages and saves 0 hours, OHS/wk says zero — correctly.

A.4 Success criteria at 30 / 60 / 90

What the operator can do, see, and feel at each stage. If any row is false, that stage hasn't shipped for that operator.

DAY 30  ── Trust earned for low-risk work
            ├── Operator runs ≥5 distinct ask types per week
            ├── Native completes ≥80% without a re-do
            ├── Operator has issued ≥1 standing directive that fires correctly
            └── Daily Pulse opened ≥3 days/wk

DAY 60  ── Routines own themselves
            ├── ≥3 standing routines fire without prompt
            ├── Feedback applied within same session ≥90% of time
            ├── Operator delegates a recurring weekly task end-to-end
            └── At least one "I would have forgotten that" save logged

DAY 90  ── Native is part of the operating rhythm
            ├── OHS/wk in 15-20 band for two consecutive weeks
            ├── Operator references Native in conversations with their team
            ├── Capability discovery surfaces ≥1 new use the operator adopts
            └── Drop-off risk indicators flat or declining

B. Input & Intent — from utterance to journey (§2.F.B carried verbatim)

Every Native interaction starts with one moment: the operator says something, Native decides what it is. Get that moment wrong and the rest of the journey is wasted motion. This section covers how input arrives, how Native reads intent, what carries across turns, and the trust handshake when Native isn't sure.

B.1 Input modalities

Native accepts input through channels the operator already uses. No new app, no new habit.

ModalityStatusBest forOperator example
Text chatDay 1Default for everything — explicit, replay-able, easy to correct."Draft the partner-update email for this week."
Slack mentionDay 1In-team asks, async hand-offs, capturing a direction mid-meeting."@native track this churn risk and tell me Friday."
Email forwardDay 1Hand off an inbound thread for Native to summarize, reply-draft, or file.Forward investor email with subject "what's the ask here?"
Scheduled promptDay 1Standing routines firing on a clock — the operator wrote the prompt once."Every Monday 8am: pulse of last week + top 3 today."
VoiceFutureHands-busy capture, car / walk / commute, raw thought dump."Hey Native, remind me to follow up with Benzy on Paisa pricing."
ScreenshotFuture"What does this mean / what should I do with this" against a captured screen.Screenshot of error toast — "is this the same one as yesterday?"

B.2 Intent recognition — the 5 types

Every utterance resolves to one of five intent types. The tell is what distinguishes one from another even when the words look similar.

IntentExample utteranceThe tell
Task"Draft a reply to Vinit's last email."
"Pull the Q2 revenue from the sheet and put it in the deck."		Concrete verb + concrete object. Operator wants a thing done, not learned.
Query"What did we decide about Paisa pricing last week?"
"How many T4 users opened the plugin yesterday?"		Question shape OR "tell me / find me / what's". Wants an answer, not an action.
Directive"From now on, always cite sources in research output."
"Stop emailing me on weekends unless it's urgent."		"From now on", "always", "never", "going forward". Changes future behavior, not this turn.
Feedback"That summary missed the actual decision."
"Too long. Cut by half next time."		References Native's prior output. Implicit or explicit "you did X — do Y instead".
New Idea"What if Native could read my calendar and pre-brief every meeting?"
"Explore using OHS/wk to score departments."		"What if", "explore", "could we", "I'm wondering". Not a request to act yet — a request to think.

B.3 Multi-turn context — what carries across

Native is not stateless. Across turns in the same session and across sessions in the same week, the following persist:

  • Recent intent. The last 3-5 things the operator asked, so "do that again for the other client" resolves.
  • Named entities. People, companies, projects the operator has talked about — so "Vinit" doesn't need a re-introduction.
  • Active charter. Standing directives the operator has issued (tone, channels, off-hours rules, who they trust on what).
  • Deferred follow-ups. Things Native promised to surface later ("remind me Friday") — they re-enter context at the right moment, not earlier.
  • Open questions. If Native asked the operator something and they didn't answer, that question stays warm for the next turn.

B.4 The trust moment — confirm-back below threshold

When Native is confident, it acts. When it isn't, it confirms before it acts. The operator never gets surprised by what Native thought they meant.

ConfidenceWhat operator sees
High (clear intent + clear object)Native proceeds. Confidence shown only if operator has asked to see it.
Medium (intent clear, object ambiguous)"Got it — you mean the Paisa partner email, not the Testlify one?" Yes/no, then proceed.
Low (intent itself unclear)"I read this as a task (draft the email) — is that right, or did you want me to just find the last one?"
Very low (can't parse)"I'm not sure what to do with this. Are you asking me to act, answer, or remember something?"
