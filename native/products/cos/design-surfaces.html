D. Outputs & channels — how Native talks back (§2.F.D)

Operator hires Native to do work, not to send messages. Output discipline: land the right shape, on the right channel, at the right time. Default to least-noise, escalate only when the operator's stated cadence or the stakes demand it.

D.1 Response modalities. Six shapes. Native picks one (sometimes two) per response. Operator can pin a default per ask type or per channel.

ModalityShapeWhen Native picks it
M1 — Text replyInline answer in the same surface the operator opened.Default. Query answers, short task confirmations, conversational follow-ups.
M2 — Slack pingDM to operator or post to a named channel.Operator is not in chat right now and asked for Slack delivery, OR a standing routine completed and the team needs to see it.
M3 — EmailPlain email, subject line carries the headline.External-facing draft, long-form synthesis, anything that needs to live in a thread the operator forwards.
M4 — Summary cardCompact card: headline · 3 bullets · one link · timestamp.Status update after a multi-step task. Daily Pulse entries. "Here's what I did" closers.
M5 — Approval-ask cardCard with the planned action, the reversibility note, and Approve / Adjust / Cancel.Native is about to do something with real-world side effects the operator hasn't pre-blessed.
M6 — Scheduled reminderFuture ping at a stated time, carrying the original context.Operator said "remind me", or Native is holding an idea until the right moment per Journey 5.

D.2 Sync, scheduled, async. Three landing times. Operator feels the difference; Native picks based on the ask shape.

Landing timeBehavior
SyncSame turn, same surface. Queries and quick tasks. Operator is waiting.
ScheduledLands at a stated moment (08:00 digest, "ping me Friday", "remind me when the doc is signed"). Operator is not waiting; the clock or a trigger is.
AsyncLands when the work finishes — could be 30 seconds or 30 minutes. Long tasks, research, multi-step routines. Native confirms receipt up front so the operator knows it's underway.

D.3 Channel selection. Three rules, applied in order. First match wins.

  1. Operator said where. If the ask carries an explicit channel ("send to Slack #ops", "email this to me", "DM me when done"), use that.
  2. Standing preference. If the operator has set a default for this ask type or this time-of-day, use that.
  3. Default to origin. Otherwise, reply on the surface the ask came in on. Chat-in → chat-out. Email-in → email-out.

Escalation override: if the matter is time-sensitive AND the default channel is async, Native promotes the channel one step (chat → Slack DM → SMS-tier ping) and notes why.

E. Personalization & memory (§2.F.E)

Native gets sharper the longer an operator uses it. Two mechanisms: skill mode (how Native talks to this operator) and memory (what Native carries forward between sessions).

E.1 Skill modes. Three modes. Native infers the mode from how the operator phrases asks, how often they correct, and how much scaffolding they consume. Operator can set it explicitly ("treat me as expert on legal, novice on code").

ModeOperator feelHow Native adjusts
SM1 — Novice"Walk me through it. I want to understand before I approve."Fuller wording. Names the steps before doing them. Confirms before any irreversible action. Explains source and reasoning in answers. Offers the next-step suggestion explicitly.
SM2 — Intermediate"Tell me the headline, do the obvious thing, ask only when it forks."Compact wording. Confirms only at branches. Reports back with summary cards, not narration. Shows source on request, not by default.
SM3 — Expert"Just do it. Surface only what I would want to override."Terse output. Acts on reasonable defaults without confirmation. Logs what it did rather than asking permission. Confirms only for high-stakes or irreversible moves.

Mode is per-domain, not global. Same operator can be SM3 on calendar, SM1 on contracts. Native carries the per-domain reading and can show the operator their current map on request.

E.2 Charter & declared preferences. The charter is what the operator has explicitly told Native about how they want to be served. Native treats charter entries as durable until revoked.

Charter entryHolds
CadenceWhen the operator wants to hear from Native — daily digest time, quiet hours, weekly retro window.
Hands-off levelPer ask class, how much autonomy is granted. ("Calendar: just do it. Money out: always ask.")
Communication styleLength tolerance, formality, language, whether to lead with the recommendation or the rationale.
Channel defaultsWhich modality lands where (see §D.3 above).
People & relationshipsWho matters, who they are to the operator, how they should be addressed.

E.3 What Native remembers. Memory is layered. Each layer has different staying power and different visibility to the operator.

LayerHoldsLifespan
Working memoryThe current thread of asks, drafts in flight, unresolved clarifications.Session, with handoff to the next session if the thread is open.
Named entitiesPeople, companies, projects, products the operator has mentioned with a stable identity.Until the operator says forget, or the entity goes inactive for 90 days and the operator confirms archive.
Trusted namesWho can act on the operator's behalf, who is allowed to receive what.Durable. Changes require explicit operator confirmation.
Prior intentsWhat the operator has asked for before, with the result. Used for "do that again" and for capability discovery.Rolling. Older entries summarize; details fall off after the operator stops referencing them.
Ratified directivesStanding rules from Journey 3 — "from now on, X".Until revoked. Revocation is a single ask.

E.4 What Native forgets.

ForgottenRule
Sensitive ephemeral contentAnything the operator marked one-time (passwords, OTPs, draft messages they discarded).
Expired chartersAnything time-bounded ("for this quarter only") drops out when the window closes.
On requestAnything the operator asks to forget. The forget command is honored immediately and visibly confirmed.

E.5 Curation surface. Operator can ask at any moment: "what do you remember about X?" and get back a structured view — entities, prior intents, standing directives, charter entries touching X. From the same view they can forget Y, correct Z, or pin this as durable. The operator never has to guess what Native is carrying.

F. Trust & authority moments — when Native asks before acting (§2.F.F — carried intact)

Native runs on a simple promise: act on what's safe and known, ask on what's risky or new. The operator should never be surprised by an action they didn't authorize, and never be nagged for an action they already blessed. Trust is the dial.

F.1 When Native asks before acting. Four trigger classes pull Native into "ask first" mode. Everything else, Native acts and reports.

TriggerWhat the operator seesWhy it asks
High-risk action"Before I send this to your full team, confirm — send / edit / cancel."Spend, send, share, or commit that touches people or money outside Native's own scope.
Irreversible action"This can't be undone after I run it. Type 'go' to confirm."Deletion, public publication, external notification, contract change. Two-step is the minimum.
New authority"You haven't asked me to do X before. Grant once / grant always / not now."First time Native is being asked to take an action it has no prior permission template for.
First-time channel or contact"First message to this person/channel — preview before I send?"Catch wrong-recipient and wrong-tone errors before they become artifacts.

F.2 Reversibility signals. Every action carries a visible reversibility tag so the operator knows what they're authorizing.

  • Reversible. "I can undo this for the next 5 minutes — say 'undo' anytime."
  • Reversible with effort. "Undoing this means I have to retract messages / re-issue / re-pay. Doable, not free."
  • Not reversible. "Once I do this, it's done. Confirming locks it in."

F.3 Audit visibility — "show me what you did and why". At any moment, the operator can ask Native to replay any past action. The operator gets back: what was asked, what Native did, why it chose that path, what it would do differently with hindsight. No log-diving, no IT tickets — same chat surface as everything else.

F.4 Trust ladder over time. Native starts conservative and earns its way to higher autonomy. The operator can also dial up or dial down explicitly at any rung.

RUNG 1 (Day 0-14)   ── ask before every external action
RUNG 2 (Day 15-45)  ── auto-act on repeated patterns; ask on novel
RUNG 3 (Day 46-90)  ── auto-act on routines; ask only on high-risk/irreversible
RUNG 4 (Day 90+)    ── runs standing routines fully; surfaces what it did, not what it'll do

G. Errors & recovery — when things go sideways (§2.F.G)

Things break. Native's job in failure is the same as in success: be honest about what happened, give the operator a clean way forward, and remember the miss so it doesn't repeat.

G.1 Misclassified intent. Native thought the operator wanted X. Operator wanted Y. Before acting on anything ambiguous, Native surfaces its read.

operator: "send the wedding update to the family group"
native:   "i think you mean — draft + send to 'Family WhatsApp', not 'Family Email'. yes / no / clarify"
   |
   |-- yes ------> proceed
   |-- no -------> "tell me which group, or paste a name"
   |-- clarify --> native asks one focused question, no more

G.2 Failed action. The action couldn't complete. Native surfaces the failure plainly, in the operator's words, with three options. No stack traces, no codes.

Operator seesOptions
"Couldn't reach Slack just now."retry now · try again in 10 min · cancel
"The doc is locked by someone else."wait and retry · make a copy · cancel
"Half the contacts received it, half didn't."retry the misses · show me which · leave as-is

G.3 Operator override — stop, undo, different approach.

OverrideNative's behavior
"stop"Native halts the in-flight action where it can, reports what completed and what didn't, asks nothing further.
"undo"Native reverses the last action if reversible; if not, it says so and offers the next-best recovery (retract, re-send correction, manual revert path).
"different approach"Native discards its current plan, re-reads the original ask, proposes a fresh plan, waits for go.

G.4 "Wrong answer, try again" loop. When the operator pushes back on a result, Native does not just re-roll. It asks what was wrong — facts, tone, scope, recipient, timing — applies the correction, and runs once more. Two consecutive misses on the same ask escalate: Native stops trying and asks the operator to walk it through what right looks like.

G.5 Learning from the correction. Every correction the operator gives is visible back to them — "I noted that. I won't make the same call on Slack channel naming next time." If the correction is general enough to be a standing rule, Native asks if the operator wants it captured as a directive. The miss is never silent and never repeated without surface.

H. Capability discovery + Daily Pulse (§2.F.H)

Two affordances solve the same operator problem from different sides: I don't know what to ask for. Discovery answers it on demand. Daily Pulse answers it before the operator opens the surface.

H.1 "What can you do for me?" Operator can ask the question in any phrasing and get a structured capability map — not a feature list. The map is organized by job, not by tool, and is filtered to what's relevant to this operator's domain and stage.

ViewWhat the operator sees
By jobGrouped under the five JTBDs — Task, Query, Directive, Feedback, New Idea — with concrete example asks for each.
By domainGrouped by area of the operator's life — calendar, inbox, research, drafting, hiring, finance, people — showing what Native already touches and what it can pick up.
By stageWhat's live for this operator today, what's available but unused, what unlocks once the operator grants a connection or sets a preference.

H.2 Proactive capability surfacing. Native does not dump the catalog. It surfaces a capability at the moment it would have helped. Three triggers:

TriggerBehavior
Adjacent askOperator just finished a task; an adjacent capability would close the loop. ("I drafted the email — want me to schedule the follow-up too?")
Repeated manualOperator has done the same manual sequence three times. Native offers to take it over as a standing routine.
Dormant fitOperator hasn't used a capability that matches their current goal. Surfaced once, gently, with an opt-in.

Capability suggestions never block. They appear as a soft offer next to the current output, and decay if the operator declines twice.

H.3 Daily Pulse. Lands at the operator's stated morning time. One screen. Designed to be skimmed in under 60 seconds and acted on with one tap per item.

+----------------------------------------------------------+
| DAILY PULSE                          Mon 08:00            |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
| YESTERDAY ── done                                         |
|   3 routines fired clean · 2 drafts sent · 1 saved hour   |
|                                                           |
| OPEN ── needs you                                         |
|   1) Approve send: vendor renewal reply  [Approve][Edit]  |
|   2) Pick: 2 meeting times for Anjali    [A][B][Other]    |
|                                                           |
| SUGGESTED ── if you want                                  |
|   - Convert "Friday investor update" to standing routine? |
|   - Forward weekend signups to your Monday review?        |
|                                                           |
| SIGNAL ── this week                                       |
|   OHS:  11.4 hrs / target 12   ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░                |
|   Re-do rate:  6%              good                       |
|                                                           |
| ASK ANYTHING                                              |
+----------------------------------------------------------+

Four blocks, fixed order. Yesterday earns trust by showing closed loops. Open is the only block that asks for the operator's attention — each item is one tap to clear. Suggested is the proactive surface from H.2. Signal shows the OHS/wk reading and the rework rate so the operator can feel Native getting better. The operator can dismiss any block permanently, reorder them, or collapse the Pulse to a single-line headline.

H.4 Weekly retro. Once a week, at a quieter moment, Native sends a longer-form retro: what got done, what got dropped, which directives fired most, which suggestions the operator accepted, and one proposed change to how Native is serving them. The retro is the operator's chance to retune the charter without having to ask for the meeting.

I. Lifecycle — onboarding to mature operator (§2.F.I)

The operator's relationship with Native moves through five stages. Each stage has a shape, a felt experience, and a failure mode if Native gets it wrong.

StageWhat happens
I.1 First session (~10 min)Three things in this order: a light charter setup (who the operator is, what their company does, what they spend their day on), a quick persona read (P1 founder, P2 ops lead, P3 solo, P4 COO-at-client), and a first real job. Not a tutorial — a real ask the operator was going to do anyway. By minute 10, Native has completed one piece of actual work and the operator has felt the loop once.
I.2 Day 1What shipped: the first job from session one, plus 1-3 quick follow-ups the operator threw at it the rest of the day. What's queued: standing directives the operator mentioned but haven't yet fired ("from now on, summarize my Slack catch-up every morning" — captured, scheduled, not yet run).
I.3 Week 1The charter ratifies — Native has enough texture on the operator to stop asking foundational questions. The first 2-3 standing directives ratify and start firing on schedule. The first Daily Pulse lands and the operator opens it. By end of week, the operator has used at least three distinct ask types and re-used at least one.
I.4 Month 1The mature loop is established: ask, act, report, learn. OHS/wk lands in the 3-6 band. The operator has had at least one "Native saved me from a mistake" moment — a wrong recipient caught, a fact corrected, a forgotten follow-up surfaced in time. Native runs ≥1 standing routine end-to-end without prompt.
I.5 Day 90+ (mature)Habit-formed. Native runs a real slice of the operator's day. OHS/wk lives in the 15-20 band. The operator references Native in conversations with their team ("Native sent that") — the most reliable signal that it's part of how they work, not a tool they visit.

I.6 Drop-off triggers + re-engagement.

Drop-off signalNative's recovery move
Daily Pulse unopened 3 days in a rowShrink pulse to 3 lines, re-anchor to highest-OHS item
No new asks in 5 daysSurface a dormant capability the operator hasn't tried, framed against a current pain
Operator started doing a task manually they used to delegateAsk one direct question: "you did X yourself yesterday — did I get it wrong before?"
Operator turned off a standing routineConfirm, log why, offer a leaner version next week

If Native is doing its job, the operator stops thinking about Native and starts thinking about the work Native makes possible. That's the lifecycle's terminal state.
