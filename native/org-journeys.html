Org for the CEO — a lending company’s week L2 · IN USE

Work-order header

idband / parentstatusversionowner
org-journeysL2 THE VOCABULARY / User JourneysDRAFTv2Experience > Org (#14.2)

A digital lending company runs on Sutra. The Native pages call it "a digital lender" and, in the builders page, Credit-Ops; this document uses Credit-Ops for the same company. It lends on EMI schedules; its product story on the pages is top-up loans first, then a gold loan and a co-lending pilot (book size and customer count are not stated anywhere in the pages, so they are not stated here). It was onboarded with four standing departments: Origination, Underwriting, Servicing and Collections (the pages write Servicing/Collections; the slash is display copy here), Compliance. Underwriting's first workflow was KYC verification, now on its third version, followed 47 times with 44 passes. Servicing runs a nightly EMI reconcile that has become an engine of its own, 31 runs and 31 passes, and learned the ledger's normal variance by run 50. A disbursal above five lakh needs a second approval, which is how loan 4821 (seven lakh, sanctioned by credit officer Meera against a five lakh limit) crossed the founder's desk. Compliance ran an RBI audit as a temporary cross-cutting system that retired when the audit was filed, and an improvised RBI sampling run on last quarter's rejected applications was later registered as a workflow. Collections found a broken payment gateway, fixed it, and left behind a sensor rule and a nightly check; someone later asked for WhatsApp payment reminders, and the change went through the rule-book before it was applied. The builders page adds a house rule: every money-touching workflow carries a reconciliation step and a check countersigned by Compliance. Every piece of work in the company is filed to a department under a charter. The Org area is where the CEO sees all of that as one tree. ### 0b. How to read this page. Part 1 is for the CEO: ten moments in a week, each with what happened in the company, what the CEO does, a low-fidelity wireframe of what they see, and why it matters. Part 2 is the same ten journeys with the exact engine call behind every step. Part 3 lists every journey we have already written across the Native documents, so this page sits in its family.

Questions that are not about the company's shape (is Underwriting keeping up with the 34-applicant batches, why was the bank file late again) belong to the chat and the department's own screens, not to the Org area.

S1 · The whole company

Scene. Monday, first coffee. The CEO wants the company in one look: which departments exist, what is this laptop versus the company, and the headline numbers.

What the CEO does. Opens Organization from the rail. Reads the counts line. Scans the tree. Types "Coll" to jump to Collections.

Low-fidelity wireframe: the same screen every time, one region highlighted. Counts are illustrative; rows marked * are illustrative additions (fact ledger, section 0b).

title row"Sutra" (this installation, the top of everything) with one line of counts: departments live and retired, charters, filed work
tree, first groupThis machine, muted and folded: the laptop's own imports, never confused with the company
tree, second groupCredit-Ops, open one level: Origination, Underwriting, Servicing and Collections, Compliance
role labelCEO of Credit-Ops (the organisation, never the machine)
filtertyping narrows the tree; a matching department keeps its parents visible

Why it matters. The machine and the company are told apart on sight, and the numbers on screen are the company record's numbers.  Under the hood → J1

S2 · What changed since last week

Scene. Last week the temporary RBI sweep handed its work back and sunset (stated in the builders page), and, for this example, a Treasury department was created under Credit-Ops. The CEO was travelling.

What the CEO does. Clicks Changes. Reads the events by day, the company's events apart from the machine's. Opens the retirement row to see the reason and where its work went.

Low-fidelity wireframe: the same screen every time, one region highlighted. Counts are illustrative; rows marked * are illustrative additions (fact ledger, section 0b).

Changes panelevents newest first, grouped by day: "Compliance: RBI sweep (temporary) retired, reason: filed, work moved to Compliance" (illustrative wording of a real event kind); "Credit-Ops: Treasury created" (illustrative)
each rowdepartment, what happened, before and after when the record carries it
status line"record complete from 12 Sep 2026" (the date the record was restored; stated, never hidden)

Why it matters. The CEO catches up in a minute and never wonders whether a department quietly vanished.  Under the hood → J2

S3 · The department card

Scene. The RBI visit is Thursday. The CEO opens Compliance to know exactly what is real before the regulator asks.

What the CEO does. Selects Compliance. Reads the card top to bottom. Opens "more" to see which facts the system does not have yet.

Low-fidelity wireframe: the same screen every time, one region highlighted. Counts are illustrative; rows marked * are illustrative additions (fact ledger, section 0b).

card, live rowsIdentity (name, path, description); Lifecycle (active since onboarding); Relations (parent: Credit-Ops; sub-departments: none); Placement (filed work: a count, and how many are held for review); Artifacts (a count, from the last export)
card, governancecharters owned here: the RBI audit mandate (retired, filed), the RBI sampling workflow (illustrative that Compliance owns it); charters linked here: top-up eligibility, whose check Compliance countersigns (the builders page's house rule)

Why it matters. A card that says "not sourced yet" is worth more before a regulator than a card that guesses.  Under the hood → J3

Scene. The collections agency writes: "Who owns the WhatsApp payment reminders, and under which rule?"

What the CEO does. Presses Cmd+K, types "WhatsApp reminders". Picks the result. Reads the department, the charter it was filed under, and how confidently it was filed.

Low-fidelity wireframe: the same screen every time, one region highlighted. Counts are illustrative; rows marked * are illustrative additions (fact ledger, section 0b).

S7 · What needs my yes

Scene. Friday. Two things wait in the Org inbox: a routine that would email the nightly EMI reconcile summary at seven, and a code change to the pricing engine's bounds check (both illustrative). The rule in one line: this inbox holds consent over the machinery and its rules (routines, code changes); money decisions travel with the work and reach the CEO in the chat, as loan 4821's second approval did; structural changes (a move, a new department) stay preview-and-hand-off in v1 and never appear here.

What the CEO does. Clicks Approvals. Reads each request and its rationale. Approves the routine, rejects the code change with a note. Sees last week's expired request marked expired.

Low-fidelity wireframe: the same screen every time, one region highlighted. Counts are illustrative; rows marked * are illustrative additions (fact ledger, section 0b).

Approvals paneleach request: what it is, who asked, how long until it expires
one request openthe arguments and the rationale, Approve and Reject
row state pillapproved, rejected, failed, or expired ("expired, no action taken")
note"org changes are not requestable here yet"

Why it matters. One inbox for consent over the machinery, and nothing applies without it.  Under the hood → J7

Scene. The new risk head wants Collections to report to Underwriting, where credit risk sits. The CEO wants to know what would break before agreeing.

What the CEO does. On Servicing and Collections, chooses "Preview a move". Picks Underwriting as the target. Reads the checks. Saves the draft with a one-line rationale. Copies the command for the operations lead.

Low-fidelity wireframe: the same screen every time, one region highlighted. Counts are illustrative; rows marked * are illustrative additions (fact ledger, section 0b).

What the CEO does. On Servicing and Collections, chooses "New sub-department". Reads the notice. Copies the command, or says it in a Sutra chat. Sees the department the next time the tree loads.

Low-fidelity wireframe: the same screen every time, one region highlighted. Counts are illustrative; rows marked * are illustrative additions (fact ledger, section 0b).

Why it matters. A designed boundary reads as a rule, not as a broken button; the department arrives with its dimensions.  Under the hood → J10
