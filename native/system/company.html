From one step to a company A READING · RULED, UNTESTED

To be re-derived. On 2026-09-26 the founder moved the world to later and redefined five capabilities; these pictures still follow the earlier order. The design they will be re-derived from is from capabilities to a system that runs.

Every part of a company starts as the smallest thing that works: one input artifact, one piece of work, one output artifact, born with the plumbing. It then grows in the ruled order: what is ruled now makes one department run, what is ruled next grows it, and what is ruled later joins departments into a company. Some parts end up large and some stay young; they meet at artifacts, through the same contract.

Founder's directions, 2026-09-26: start small with one input and one output and grow it until a full system has every dimension, parts grown and small connecting through a minimum of plumbing; then derive this growth from the ruled order and merge it under the system, grown in this order. This page replaces from one step to a company (archived), whose steps followed size rather than the ruled order. The kill line is in section 6.

1 · The seed, and the plumbing it is born with

Step 1 · The seed: the loan book, Intake, the due list, inside a department whose identity exists from birth. Under each artifact, what every artifact carries; above the work, the recipe every piece of work is born with. The strip shows all six families present and pale. This already works, and it can already connect to anything that speaks the contract.

The waist. Above it, work of every kind at every size of growth; below it, every kind of artifact and store. Everything meets through one thin contract that only moves and records artifacts, the way every application on the internet meets every network through one small protocol. Goals, checks, gates, caps and triggers are not in the waist: each piece of work carries its own.

3 · Now, next, later: from one step to a company

The same collections department, zoomed out, in the order ruled on what comes first. The step-by-step view of the same growth, one capability per picture, is the system, grown in this order.

Now · the department that runs

After the 16 steps ruled now: the seed plus Call borrower, the first work that leaves the record, and the reply that comes back as an artifact. Nothing has grown yet: every strip is pale. The kit each piece carries is exactly the now tier: named reads and writes, goal, check, versions, run log, lineage, auto inside, ask and stamp, deny, cap, chain brake, undo, manual, schedule, wake on a new version.

Next · the department grows

The capabilities ruled next arrive: the due list forks to Score risk and Plan outreach, Coordinate merges them at a junction that publishes when both are in, replies are recorded against a ledger that holds one live promise per loan, and the review reads back into the rules. The first families turn solid, each where its own record asked: EDGE at the junction, GOVERN on the call, GROW on the review.

Months later · uneven depth

Each piece grew only what its record asked for. Call borrower grew most; Plan outreach, run the same way again and again, became code: a tool; Intake only found its daily beat and stayed small.

Later · the company

Ruled later: departments reading each other's artifacts, and the functions that appear at scale. Underwriting is partly grown, collections is grown, finance is young; they meet only at shared artifacts, through the same contract.

4 · What the fields already know

Gall's law, and why it holds

John Gall, General Systemantics, 1975 (reissued as Systemantics, 1977); Herbert Simon, The Architecture of Complexity, Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society, 1962 · confidence: high on the sources; Gall's is a heuristic, Simon's the argument

Gall's aphorism: a complex system that works is invariably found to have evolved from a simple system that worked. Simon gives the reason: complex systems form far faster when they are built through stable intermediate forms, each of which works on its own.

What it gives Native: every step on this page is a stable intermediate form: the seed works, the chain works, the junction works, before anything else is added.

The narrow waist

National Research Council, Realizing the Information Future, 1994; Micah Beck, On the hourglass model, Communications of the ACM, 2019; Akhshabi and Dovrolis, SIGCOMM, 2011 · confidence: high on the sources; moderate on the mapping

One thin layer in the middle, with many things above and below it, lets both sides change on their own. Beck argues the waist must be minimally sufficient: no more than the things above it need. Akhshabi and Dovrolis show such a waist tends to emerge, and then to freeze.

What it gives Native: the artifact contract is the waist: kept minimal, and changed only as a one-way door.

Where functions belong

Saltzer, Reed and Clark, End-to-end arguments in system design, ACM Transactions on Computer Systems, 1984 · confidence: high

A function that only the ends can do correctly belongs at the ends, not in the shared layer between them.

What it gives Native: the goal, check, gate, cap and trigger are carried by each piece of work, not built into the contract; the contract only moves and records artifacts.

Same kit, different reading

Lewis Wolpert, Positional information and the spatial pattern of cellular differentiation, Journal of Theoretical Biology, 1969 · confidence: high on the source; the growth reading is an analogy

Every cell carries the same genome; what it becomes depends on where it sits.

What it gives Native: every piece carries the same kit from birth, and what it grows depends on the terrain its record shows; Wolpert is about position, not growth over time, so this is an analogy.

Parts at different stages, linked

Simon Wardley, Wardley maps, from about 2005 · confidence: high on the source; note the direction is the opposite of 'grown' here

Every component of a business sits somewhere from genesis to commodity; components at different stages coexist and link. In Wardley's sense the evolved end is the standard, boring one.

What it gives Native: the contract belongs at the commodity end; a part that settles and crystallizes into a tool moves towards it.

Piecemeal growth

Christopher Alexander and others, The Oregon Experiment, 1975 · confidence: moderate

A campus grows well through many small local acts, each repairing what a diagnosis of use shows is needed, under a few shared patterns, rather than through a master plan.

What it gives Native: growth is pulled piece by piece by the record, under the shared contract and rules.

The strongest counter: growth is contagious along the links. When Call borrower grows strict about whom it may call, it needs Intake to leave out paid and paused loans; either Intake grows with no signal in its own record, or Call borrower re-reads the loan book and Intake becomes dead weight. The answer: growth travels upstream as rejects. Call borrower rejects a due-list version that holds a paused loan; the reject is written into Intake's record; Intake's check grows because its reader asked, visibly and stamped. The small part grows exactly as much as its readers need, and no more.

Small parts and grown parts meet at artifacts. The contract is thin and fixed; everything else is carried by each piece and grown where its record asks, stamped, and able to step down.
