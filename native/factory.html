The factory: what we are building, and how DRAFT v2

A company runs on routines that live in people's heads. We are building the way to hand those routines to machines, one at a time, without the owner losing control. After that, a routine improves because its own written record says what to change, not because someone remembered.

1 · What we are trying to do

This is not intelligence. A machine that can think about anything (what people call AGI) is a better craftsman. We are building the factory around the craftsman: the logbook, the checks, the foreman, the written agreements where one machine hands to the next, and the owner's stamp. A smarter worker still has to be checked. The checking is what we build.
A machine that thinksThe factory
Answerscan a machine think about anythingcan a company hand its routines to any machine without losing control
Assumes about the workerit is smartit is fallible and replaceable: a person today, a checklist tomorrow, code next year, a smarter model after that
Value compoundsin the model, for everyonein the company's own record, for that company only; the method can be copied to another company, the company's own logbook cannot

Every feature below answers one of six questions. A feature that answers none of them is not ours to build.

#The questionThe answerFeature
1What is the smallest thing I can hand to a machine?one piece of work: say what done is, do one thing, check itF1
2How do I know it did it right?the check, and a line in the logbook every timeF1, F2
3When can I let it run without me?when the logbook shows the same result again and again, and I stamp itF3
4What happens when two of them must work together?a foreman decides who goes first; a spec sheet says what passes between themF4, F5, F8
5How does it get better without me pushing?it reads its own logbook, proposes one change, I stamp or refuseF6
6What can it never do?act outside its rules, rewrite its own rules, or change how it works without my stamp. It may do the work; it may not change the workF7

2 · The six stages every routine moves through

A routine moves one stage at a time and back whenever the evidence turns bad. Writing it down (stage 1) is free. From stage 2 on, every step forward needs logbook evidence and the owner's stamp. Stages 0 to 3 are one ladder, hardening. Stages 4 and 5 are not higher rungs of it: a routine can be at 3, 4 and 5 at once. The order is the reading order for a customer, and the order we build in.

StageFactory wordsAnswersWhat it needs to enterWhat you get

1 Written down with a checka job cardQ1, Q2say what done is; a check that can tellit can be handed to anyone; every run leaves a line in the logbook

Question 6, what it can never do, is not a stage. It is the gate on every arrow. Scaling, the workshop growing a foreman and the other four seats, is not a stage either: it happens to a whole department at any stage, once several routines share something.

Blue is where a routine starts paying back hours.

4 · The dials: how each axis is used, in the factory

An axis is one dial on the plant's instrument panel. Some dials the order sets before a job runs (shape). Some the logbook moves as a routine proves itself (grow). One gates every move (govern). Three are read on every run (edges, stores, the clock). And the move is how a new dial gets minted when a question has no dial yet. Each row: what the dial reads in the record's words, how the factory sees it, which of the six questions it serves, and whether we read it today.

Six groups of dials. The order sets the orange ones; the logbook moves the blue ones; the stamp sits on the ask position.
