Automation and India HUMAN AND AGENT · research, level 1

How much of the economy the known references expect AI to automate, where they agree and disagree, the assumptions this document adopts, and a first-level model of the markets that open in India given how India's economy is built.

How to read: the answer comes first; every number has its source beside it; every assumption is in an orange box with a letter, so it can be argued with one at a time. Projection marks our own numbers. This is level 1: the frame, the references and the model's shape; the sizing is a first pass to be replaced by measured runs.

The answer

  1. The references agree on direction and disagree on size. Between a quarter and two-fifths of today's jobs are exposed; the value runs from about 3 to 16 trillion USD a year by 2030 depending on what is counted.
  2. For India the published number is 1.2 to 1.5 trillion USD cumulative by FY30 (EY), with 69% of it in business services, finance, education, retail and health.
  3. India's structure is not the structure those global numbers assume. Informal work is about 90% of employment, MSMEs are 31% of GDP, services are 55% of value added, software exports are 221 billion USD a year and 54% of them go to the United States. The first market that opens is not "automate the office": it is "give the firm an office for the first time".
  4. Five markets open first in India: the office for the informal firm; the front door for the clinic, school and shop in the customer's language; the conversion of the IT-BPM sector's own work; agent orgs built on the digital public rails; and market linkage for the farm. Each is sized at first level below with its assumptions.
  5. The exposed sector is also the opportunity. India's 221 billion USD of software exports is the most exposed line in the country and the most able to sell the replacement.

Why this chart: why the dots spread, and what we take from them
  • Claim. The references do not disagree about whether; they disagree about the yardstick.
  • Top row. "Exposed" (IMF, Goldman) means some tasks of the job can be done by AI; "disrupted" (WEF) means the job changes or goes; "automatable hours" (McKinsey) counts time. The same economy gives 22% to 66% depending on the word.
  • Bottom row. McKinsey counts generative AI productivity in 63 use cases; Goldman counts the GDP lift over a decade; PwC counts all AI and adds 9.1 trillion of consumption effects to 6.6 trillion of productivity. Not one number three ways; three questions.
  • What we take. For a market model the useful line is McKinsey's: a share of hours, by function, that a machine can do. That is the same sorting the share chapter uses: can a machine check it, does it need a body, must someone be liable.

3. The assumptions this document adopts

A1. Hours, not jobs. We model work as hours that a machine can check (McKinsey's 29.5% of US hours is the anchor). A job is a bundle of hours; the person keeps the bundle's seat.
A2. India at the IMF emerging-economy level. 40% of jobs exposed, adjusted down for the informal share: we assume only formal, recorded work can be automated in a first wave, so the first addressable base is the formal tenth of employment plus the recorded part of MSME output.
A3. Low exposure is a market, not a ceiling. Where no office exists, the first product is the office itself: records, orders, bills, a site, a front door. This is the organic department on Sutra (run 5) sold to a firm that never had one.
A4. The rails exist. UPI (21.6 billion transactions in December 2025), Udyam registration (8.7 crore), Account Aggregator and ONDC are assumed available to an agent org as data and payment rails without a new integration per firm.
A5. Price per seat, not per licence. An agent org is paid as a small monthly seat plus tokens, so the addressable count is firms and clinics and shops, not employees.
A6. Language is not a barrier by H1. The customer-facing part of an agent org works in the customer's language; the EY readiness gap (75% of C-suite low to moderate readiness) is a sales cost, not a technical one.
A7. Software exports are the exposed line. BPO services, the dominant part of ITES exports, are the Indian work most like McKinsey's automatable hours. We treat the 221 billion USD as both at risk and as the sales force for the replacement.
A8. Sizing is first level. Each market below is base times share times price, with the share an assumption until rung 5 of the experiment measures it.

4. The global market structure, in one line

Four layers settle: models (a few vendors), harnesses (a handful), agent orgs (millions), and markets between agent orgs (late in the shift). The money sits at the top and the bottom; the control sits in the agent org layer. The argument is on Futures at H1; Gartner's 40% of enterprise apps with agents by end 2026 and 450 billion USD of agentic software revenue by 2035 are the layer below agent orgs growing.

Why this chart: the informal bar is the market, not the obstacle
  • Claim. The usual reading, that India is less exposed and so less of a market, is backwards for agent orgs.
  • Why. The IMF's exposure counts tasks already done in offices. In India most work is done without a record, an order book or a bill. An agent org does not replace those; it is the first time they exist. That is what the website department did for a tailor and an organic farm in the Human Simulation runs.
  • Evidence. about 90% informal employment (PLFS); 69% within services (NITI Aayog); 8.7 crore Udyam registrations, so the firms are countable; UPI at 21.6 billion transactions a month, so they can be paid.
  • What would change it. If informal firms will not pay a monthly seat (assumption A5 fails), the market is the formal tenth only and the first-level sizes below shrink by an order of magnitude.

Projection So what: the markets that open in India are the office that never existed (1), the front door in the customer's language (2), and the conversion of India's own export work (3). The rails (4) make the first two cheap to reach; the farm (5) is where agent orgs first need each other. The global estimates size the hours in offices that exist; India's size is in the offices that do not.

Why this chart: why start bottom-left when the money is top-right
  • Claim. The order to enter is the order of measurability, because every size above is an assumption until a run measures it.
  • Why. Market 1 can be measured this month: the website department exists, the goal files exist, the return line (N-023) is the test. Market 3 needs a BPO client's stamp on a live process and a contract; a year at least.
  • Evidence. The Human Simulation runs: a tailor, a farm, a clinic's listing, a trip, each a department in minutes. None yet with a paying owner, which is the first measurement.
  • What would change it. A BPO client who wants market 3 first and will stamp the process: then the order flips and the money arrives before the measurements.
