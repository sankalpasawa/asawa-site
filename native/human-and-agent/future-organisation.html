The future organisation HUMAN AND AGENT

Every person with an agent org, the work moving in three waves, and the frame under it: six levels, the wants loop, where memory and understanding live.

Why this picture: three waves, in this order, and why the person's box never empties
  • Claim. Work leaves people for their agent orgs in a fixed order, and what never leaves is the seat: the want, the stamp, the rule, the liability.
  • Why wave 1 is paperwork. Search, drafting, booking and billing are the work a machine can check against a closed list. On Sutra that is what engines already do: a site from words in 4 minutes, a web search filed with its sources (Human Simulation runs 3 and 5, 2026-09-29/30).
  • Why wave 2 is the manager. Deciding who does what and when is the work Adaptation and Priority already do in a department: a line of engines shaped from the owner's words, reordered by one sentence. The manager's box empties before the maker's.
  • Why wave 3 is other organisations. One agent org asking another for a filing needs a market: who may call whom, a price, a promise with a time. That has not run yet; it is rung 3 of the experiment.
  • Why the person's box does not empty. Three questions sort every piece of work: can a machine check it, does it need a body, must someone be liable. The want, the taste and the signature fail the first and third, so they stay.
  • What would change it. Law that lets an agent org carry liability (the seat shrinks further), or a run where an engine's work has to be finished by hand (wave 1 is slower than drawn).

The frame

Six levels, three kinds of problem

Why this picture: the levels are Native's own; the problems sit where the runs put them
  • Claim. Everything up to the department is built and solves the founder's two named problem kinds; the economic kind starts one level higher.
  • Evidence. The six levels are the ones on the structure page. Skill-level work is what engines verify against closed lists (run 1 onward). Management-level work, who does what and when, is what Adaptation and Priority did in runs 3 and 5 (a line shaped from words, one stamp).
  • Why the top is unplaced. No run has had two owners. Paying, trusting and owning only appear when a second human's AO is on the other side.
  • What would change it. A run where one department fakes or hand-bridges what another should file: then the economic problem is not at the AO level but inside the department.

The wants loop

Why this picture: one hop is seen, the second is the experiment
  • Claim. An AO that solves a human want acquires wants of its own, and those go to three places: vendors, the human, other AOs.
  • Evidence. Run 3: Identity asked for the internet, the owner stamped, Adaptation shaped a Source Reader engine. That is "AO wants, then the human seat", one hop, observed (section 9, steps 3 to 5). Run 5: a site from words with one stamp is "human wants, then AO, then outcomes".
  • Why the dashed edges. "Another AO" and "filings, prices back" have never run. They are drawn dashed because they are the claim, not the record.
  • What would change it. If an AO's wants can always be met by vendors alone, the other-AO edge disappears and the civilization is a market of vendors, not of AOs.

AO money and the meta-AO

An AO spends four stocks (tokens and compute; data and access; human time as stamps and answers; other AOs' work) and sells one thing: an outcome a human wanted, kept alive. Native already names the stock as the Resource primitive. The meta-AO is nothing new in kind: the EDGE family (who may call whom) and the RESOURCE family (price) lifted one level, plus a promise with a date (TIME) and a dispute path (GOVERN).
