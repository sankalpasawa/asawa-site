Market map at H2: one rash, one morning

The abstract chapters say what happens to every industry. This draws one: health, and inside it one aspect, a skin condition treated. Asha, 34, finds a rash on her arm at 7 am. By 9 am the medicine is at her door. Eleven parties touched it; two were people.

Flows:datamoneygoodsa human's stamp
Why this picture: a star around the person's AO, one market under everything
  • Claim. In H2 every exchange in an episode of care goes through the person's own AO, two humans act, and every AO on the map is registered in one market.
  • Why the star. Consent and the record sit with the person; the clinic, the lab and the pharmacy each get only the slice they need, through her AO. The alternative, the clinic's AO at the centre, is today's shape with the hospital holding the record.
  • Why two humans. The three sorting questions (3.4): only the signed diagnosis needs someone liable, and only the door needs a body. The pathologist and the insurer's reviewer act on exception.
  • Why the market at the bottom. Promises between AOs (a result by evening, medicine by 8:40) are worthless unless something holds them. The registry is where prices and kept promises are recorded; the regulator reads it instead of inspecting each party.
  • Why health, why a rash. The dermatology case was walked in run 3 on Sutra (section 9 is that run); a rash is small enough to draw and touches hardware, logistics, pharma and insurance.
  • What would change it. Clinics refusing cases from personal AOs (then the clinic's AO is the hub and the star flips), or no drone corridor (then step 6 is a rider and the time is 9:30, the shape unchanged).

Why these numbers, and how the H2 column was made
  • What is sourced. The latest and 2030 columns are published forecasts from named houses; where they disagree (teledermatology 41 to 67 bn) the range is shown, not a pick.
  • How H2 was projected. Each sourced 2030 figure extended at a slowing rate: teledermatology from about 18% a year to about 12%; drones kept near 25% because the corridors open late; administration cut by the sorting rule in 3.4 (claims, booking and billing are checkable, bodiless, low-liability).
  • Why these five measures. They are the five flows on the map that have a market behind them: the consult (teledermatology), the delivery (drones), the paperwork (administration), the whole (health spend), and the device (inside teledermatology's product segment).
  • What would change them. A regulator that bars AI triage from billing (teledermatology slows), or a city that bans drone corridors (drones stay a rider's job; the map's times move, its shape does not).
Why this picture: why 100 becomes 68, segment by segment
  • Clinician 45 to 15. The visit shrinks from 20 minutes to 6 because the clinic's AO drafts the plan and the person's AO brings the record and the image. The signature stays; the reading time goes.
  • Admin 12 to AO 6. Booking, notes, claims and billing are the first things the sorting rule gives to AOs; what remains is the AO's own cost: tokens and a share of the harness.
  • Tests 10 to hardware 14. A device bought once at home plus the lab's analyzer. Hardware grows because it replaces a visit, not because it gets dearer.
  • Medicine 25 to 24. Molecules do not get cheaper because an AO ordered them. This is the segment that holds, so its share of the smaller total rises.
  • Evidence. The 2026 split is a typical Indian city visit from the goal files; the H2 split follows the step table above. Both are indexes, not prices.
  • What would change it. A drug price fall (medicine shrinks), or hardware subsidised by insurers (hardware moves into the insurer's line).

Projection The H2 health market around this aspect: people about a fifth of the spend, down from half; hardware makers a fifth, up; medicine a third, steady; logistics and the market a tenth; the AOs and their tokens a tenth, new. The AO layer is small in money and large in control: it chooses, drafts and routes everything.
