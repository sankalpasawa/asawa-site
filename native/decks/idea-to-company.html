Part I, research

Before you cut anything, answer three questions

Research is not a report. It is three answers that decide where the first cut points. Without them the compass cuts the wrong goal, neatly.

QUESTION A
Who is it for?

One kind of person, their pain, what they do about it today, and what they would pay to make it go away.

QUESTION B
What must move?

The one number the company exists to move. It becomes the check on the company's goal, and every subgoal inherits it.

QUESTION C
Which first?

Of all the goals that would move the number, the one with the most impact and the firmest ground. That is where the first cut points.

THE RULE FOR RESEARCHEvery answer is a sentence with a number and a source beside it. A number without a source is an assumption, and assumptions get a letter so they can be checked later.
In the compass these three live in SHAPE (the lens: where to cut) and in Priority (the impact axis: what must move). On the Native site, Market and people and Long-term direction are still to be written.

Question B

What must move?

One number the company exists to move, readable from its own record. It becomes the check on the company's goal, and every department, engine and subgoal below inherits a piece of it.

THE COMPANY'S GOAL, WITH ITS CHECKGive small loans to salaried people the banks turn away.Done, every month, when: money in hand within 2 working days for 9 of 10 approved, and 97 of 100 repay on time.

Two numbers, because one alone can be gamed: pay out fast by lending to anyone, or repay well by lending to nobody. Together they describe the company.

Rung 2, the department

Every department is born the same way

A department is one goal with its check, five functions that keep it honest, and the engines that do its work. The same shape at every level; that is what lets one rule grow the whole company.

THE FIVE FUNCTIONS, IN PLAIN WORDSIdentity says what the department is for and what it never does. Adaptation watches the record and proposes changes. Priority says what comes first. Coordination keeps two engines from tripping over each other. Audit asks whether the whole is healthy.
THE SAME CUT, ONE RUNG DOWNOrigination's goal cut on SHAPE gives the five engines on the left. These are the five pieces from the first deck.
Collections, Finance and the others get the same five functions on their first day, mostly empty, and fill as their pain appears.

Part IV, back up the ladder

After 40 loans, the record climbs back up

GROW does not cut. It reads the record and lifts what repeated one rung up at a time, until it reaches the research questions and re-opens one of them.

This is why research is not a one-time report. The record answers the research questions better than the first interviews did, and the loop runs again.

Do it yourself

From an idea to a company, in eight moves

  1. Who is it for?One person, their pain, what they do today, what they would pay. Numbers with sources; assumptions lettered.
  2. What must move?One or two numbers readable from your own record. They become the company's check.
  3. Which first?Rank the goals by impact and firmness of ground. The first cut points there.
  4. Write the company's goal in one line, with its check.
  5. Cut on SHAPE into departments, then into engines.One goal and one check per piece, at every rung. Draw the other departments; start only the first.
  6. Cut on TIME; add EDGE, GOVERN, RESOURCE.The grid of subgoals, down to the work atom. Empty cells stay empty.
  7. Run it. Stamp only the asks.
  8. Let GROW climb.Frozen steps, rules, a moved goal, a re-opened research question. Then go to move 1 again.
THE THREE RESEARCH MOVESThey are the only moves that need the outside world. Everything after them reads the company's own record.
THE SAME MOVE, EVERY RUNGCompany to department, department to engine, engine to subgoal: take a goal with its check, ask one question, cut, give each piece its check. Nothing else is needed to grow the tree.
THE PROMISEFirst loan expensive, hundredth loan cheap, and the hundredth loan tells you who your customer really is.

Try it on someone

A case question that leads a person to this system

Give this to someone who has never seen the compass. Then ask the follow-ups in order, only when they stall. A strong answer rebuilds the first deck on its own.

THE QUESTIONA friend has just started a small lending company. One product: personal loans of 1 to 5 lakh for salaried people the banks turn away. She has three staff, a laptop, and no process. She wants a company that can give out a loan without her touching it.

Organise her work so that: (a) a loan is paid out within two working days; (b) she only looks at the things that truly need her; (c) the hundredth loan costs less to process than the first; and (d) after a hundred loans she knows better than today whom to lend to.

Draw it. You may ask me anything about her business.
If they stall, askIt leads to
Whom should she lend to, and what one number should she watch?research: who, what must move

When does each piece happen, and by when?TIME
Who does each piece, and who hands to whom?EDGE

What does each piece cost her, in minutes?RESOURCE
What should change after a hundred loans, and how would she know?GROW
Scoring: a check on the goal; pieces with their own checks; a grid with honest empty cells; named hand-offs; auto versus ask; a cost per piece; a learning loop that changes the cut. Six of seven is the compass, found alone.

In one sentence

Research says where to look. The compass cuts the goal down to the work atom. The record climbs back up and changes where to look. The owner writes one line and stamps the asks.

That loop, run at every rung, is a company.
