One goal,
many subgoals

How one line of intent becomes work a company can run, check, and improve. Told with one small loan.

Draft for the founder's read, 2026-10-12. The example is a lending company.
1

Start

The goal, in one line

A goal is only a goal when you can say how you would know it is done.

THE GOAL
Give one customer a small personal loan.
HOW WE KNOW IT IS DONE
The money is in the customer's account within 2 working days, and every step is on record.
One goal, one check. Every piece we cut from it will carry the same two things.
2

The method

How to cut a goal, in three rules

The same three rules at every size: a company, a department, one loan.

1Ask one question of the goal, and cut along the answer.
"What pieces does it have?" gives pieces. "When?" gives phases.
2Every piece gets one goal and one check.
Stop cutting when a piece cannot be split and still be work. That piece is a work atom.
3Add the next question. The pieces become a grid.
New questions do not add pieces. They add readings to the pieces you have.
In Native the questions are called axes, and the axes come in six families: the compass.
3

The compass

Six questions you can ask of any piece of work

Six families. Every question you could ask of work belongs to one of them. We will ask them one at a time.

SHAPE
What pieces does it have?

Read fresh from this goal, every time. A different goal gets a different cut.

TIME
When, and by when?

Which phase each piece lives in, and the clock it must beat.

EDGE
Who hands what to whom?

The links between pieces. Where work waits, and where it gets stuck.

GOVERN
May it go on its own?

Auto, ask, or deny. Who stamps, before the run, so nobody is surprised.

RESOURCE
What does it spend?

Minutes of a person, calls of the system, money. A finite stock.

GROW
What has the record taught?

Across many runs: what repeats becomes a rule, a template, or code.

We start with SHAPE and TIME, the two anyone can draw on paper. They alone make the goal runnable.

When does each piece happen, and by when?

11 subgoals, 9 empty cells. Two questions were enough to make the goal runnable.

Question 4 of 6, GOVERN

May this subgoal go on its own, or must someone stamp it?

Most cells run on their own. Two cells ask for a stamp. One cell can stop the whole goal.

auto: runs on its ownask: a person stamps firstdeny: the goal stops here if the check fails
The stamps are known before the run. Nobody is surprised by an ask, and nothing leaves the record without one.

Question 5 of 6, RESOURCE

What does each subgoal spend?

Two numbers per cell: minutes of a person and calls of the system. Now the goal has a cost, and the cost can be read back after every run.

One run: 22 person-minutes, 14 system calls, 2 working days. The scarcest stock is a person's attention; the two asks are where it goes. Illustrative numbers; in a live department they come from the record.

Question 6 of 6, GROW

After 40 loans, what has the record taught?

GROW does not cut. It reads the 40 runs and lifts what repeated into a rule, a template, or a step frozen to code.

First run expensive, every run cheaper. Not a hope: the grid records every cell, and GROW reads the record.

One subgoal, six readings

Take one cell: "read the bank statements and pull the credit score". It is not six things. It is one thing, read six ways, on every run.

Do it yourself

Cut any goal in five moves

  1. Write the goal in one line, with its check.If you cannot say how you would know it is done, it is a wish, not a goal.
  2. Ask SHAPE: what pieces?Follow the seams. One goal and one check per piece. Stop at the work atom.
  3. Ask TIME: when?Put each piece in its phase. Leave empty cells empty.
  4. Add one reading at a time.EDGE: who hands to whom. GOVERN: auto, ask, deny. RESOURCE: what it spends.
  5. Run it, and let GROW change the next cut.A step that never varies freezes to code. A stamp given the same way ten times becomes a rule. A pain that repeats grows a new rule of its own.
WHY TWO QUESTIONS FIRSTSHAPE and TIME are the two anyone can draw. They make the goal runnable on their own. The other four add readings, not pieces.
WHY SIX, AND WHY THEY DO NOT CHANGEEvery question you can ask of work sorts into one family. New questions are added freely inside a family. The six stay.
THE PROMISEFirst run expensive, every run cheaper, because the grid records every cell and GROW reads the record.

In one sentence

A company is work. Every piece of work can be asked six questions. The system asks them on every run, writes the answers to the record, and the record makes the next run cheaper.

The owner writes one line and stamps the asks. Everything else is the compass at work.
