ARCHIVED 2026-09-26 — Artifacts and work A READING · RULED, UNTESTED

Archived 2026-09-26. Merged on the founder's direction into the system, grown in this order. The model and where decisions are made now live on The model, under the system, grown in this order. Kept below as it was.

A company is artifacts, the information that exists, and the work between them, which turns some artifacts into others. Artifacts are the fixed points: a loan book, a plan, a reply. Work is the change: it starts at artifacts and ends at artifacts, and never edits one in place; it writes the next version. Where several pieces of work meet at one artifact there is a junction. Some work reaches out into the world, and what comes back is one more artifact.

Founder's direction, 2026-09-26: the whole company is artifacts and work; a tool is work, crystallized; two pieces of work on one artifact meet at a coordinating piece of work, and the artifact with the work around it is one level you can zoom into. The same day the picture was flipped on the founder's word, from work as the boxes to artifacts as the boxes, because information is what stays and work is what moves. Proceeding untested by choice; the kill line is in section 6.

2 · What the fields already know

Several fields built this independently. That is the best sign the idea is sound. One rival shows the road not taken.

Place/transition nets

Carl Adam Petri, dissertation, 1962; workflow nets: van der Aalst, 1997-1998 · confidence: high

A net has two kinds of node: places, which hold tokens, and transitions, which fire. A transition fires only when every place it reads holds a token; firing takes tokens from its inputs and puts tokens in its outputs. Petri drew places as circles and transitions as thin bars on the arrows, which is the flipped picture on this page. Van der Aalst used workflow nets for business processes and proved when one is sound: every case that starts can finish.

What it gives Native: artifacts are places, work is transitions; a junction's rule is the firing rule; soundness is a check the whole landscape can pass or fail before it runs.

Artifact-centric business processes

Nigam and Caswell, IBM Systems Journal, 2003; Bhattacharya, Hull et al., 2007; Hull, survey, 2008 · confidence: high on the source; moderate on the adoption claim

IBM's researchers modelled operations around business artifacts, the things a business actually tracks (an order, a claim, an application), each with its own information and life cycle, and treated the work as services that move artifacts along. They found business people read these models more easily than activity flows.

What it gives Native: the direct field for the flip: artifacts first, work as what moves them; each artifact has a life cycle, which is GROW.

Blackboard systems

Hearsay-II, Erman, Hayes-Roth, Lesser and Reddy, ACM Computing Surveys, 1980; Nii, AI Magazine, 1986 · confidence: high

Independent specialists share one blackboard; each writes a partial answer when it can add something, and a control component decides who goes next. The solution assembles itself on the board.

What it gives Native: several pieces of work contributing to one artifact is a blackboard; the Coordinate work is the blackboard's control component.

Stigmergy

Grasse, on termite nest building, 1959; Heylighen, Cognitive Systems Research, 2016 · confidence: high on Grasse; moderate on the generalisation

Termites coordinate through what they leave behind: each ball of mud invites the next. Heylighen argues Wikipedia and open-source code work the same way, each edit inviting the next.

What it gives Native: work never calls work; a new artifact is the trace that wakes the next work, which is why the landscape can grow without a central plan.

Build systems

Feldman, Make, Software: Practice and Experience, 1979 · confidence: high

Files are the nodes; rules say how to turn some files into another; a file is rebuilt only when what it depends on changed.

What it gives Native: the everyday proof that artifacts-as-nodes, work-as-edges runs at scale; it also gives the rule for when work should wake: when an input artifact has a new version.

The rival: actors

Hewitt, Bishop and Steiger, IJCAI, 1973 · confidence: high

Each actor has a mailbox and sends messages straight to other actors; there is no shared place.

What it gives Native: the design Native does not take: direct messages leave no artifact between two pieces of work, and a company needs that record.

3 · The landscape, one axis at a time

One department, a lending company's collections, drawn nine times. Each picture adds one thing and keeps everything before it. Amber boxes are artifacts, blue chips are work, the dashed green area is the world.

v1 · Bare: artifacts, and the work between them

An artifact (amber box) is information that exists: a loan book, a due list, a plan, a reply. Work (the blue chip on the arrows) turns artifacts into new artifacts; every piece of work starts at artifacts and ends at artifacts. Coordinate reads two artifacts and writes one, which is why work is drawn as a chip where the arrows meet, not as a bare line. The world is where some work reaches out and where replies come back from.

v2 · Add SHAPE: every piece of work carries its goal, its check and its impact

Before a piece of work runs, SHAPE reads its input artifacts and fixes the goal, the check that says it is done, and the impact it must move (ruled 2026-09-25). The artifacts do not change; the work carries the intent.

v3 · Add GOVERN: the gate is a property of the work

Publishing the outreach plan is routine, so Coordinate runs on auto. Calling a borrower leaves the record and cannot be undone, so that work waits for a person's stamp before it runs.

v4 · Add EDGE: the work is the edge, and a junction is an artifact with its coordinator

Two pieces of work, Score risk and Plan outreach, each write their own contribution; Coordinate merges them and publishes the outreach plan only when both are in. The plan and the work around it are one junction you can zoom into. Nothing writes the same artifact twice at once.

v5 · Add RESOURCE: what each piece of work may spend

The work that spends carries a bar: calls today for the call, model tokens for scoring and planning. Work whose bar is full waits; that is how one new artifact is kept from waking a flood of work.

v6 · Add TIME: when each piece of work wakes

Intake runs daily at 09:00; the review runs weekly; a reply is expected within 48 hours. Time decides when work wakes; the junction's rule decides whether it may.

v7 · Add GROW: artifacts keep every version

Work never edits an artifact in place; it writes the next version. The promise ledger and the outreach plan keep their history, so the review reads a history, not a snapshot. That accumulated record is what GROW measures.

v8 · Add the moves: Realization down, Reflection up

Down: any piece of work opens into its own artifacts and smaller work, and work that repeats the same way becomes a tool, work crystallized. Up: the review finds a pattern in the promises and lifts it into a department rule. The rules box is the department's Identity.

v9 · Add the stages: Decide, Delegate, Deliver, Propose

The department's rules narrow into every piece of work (Delegate). An engine runs each piece and its check flips (Deliver). A person stamps before the call leaves for the world (Decide). The review sends its rule up as an ask (Propose). The whole frame, on one landscape of artifacts and work.

4 · Where decisions are made

Artifacts do not decide; they hold what is known. Every decision is work: it reads artifacts (the facts, the options, the rules) and chooses, on the way from one artifact to the next, against the goal that piece of work carries. A decision is not a third kind of thing. The decisions that matter write their choice down as an artifact of their own, so the next piece of work can read it and anyone can later ask why.

One call, zoomed in from the landscape above. The orange marks are the five places a choice is made. The goal sits in the rules at the top; every piece of work carries its share of it as goal, check and impact; the read-back at close says whether it moved. Escalate and the escalation list are added here to show a branch.

What the fields say. Herbert Simon (The New Science of Management Decision, 1960) split a decision into intelligence, design and choice: a decision is a process, which is to say work. OMG's Decision Model and Notation (DMN 1.0, 2015) models decisions apart from the process, each with its input data and its knowledge source: here, the input artifacts and the department rules. In a Petri net, a place with two outgoing transitions is a conflict; only one fires, and a guard on the artifact decides which. Decision records (Nygard, 2011) are the practice of keeping the choice as an artifact. Confidence: high that each exists as described; moderate on the mapping, which is this page's reading.

A decision is work that writes its choice down. If anyone will later ask why, the choice needs an artifact; if no one will, it can stay inside the work.
