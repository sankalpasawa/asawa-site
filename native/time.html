Time — schedule + dynamics OVERVIEW · sub-page

WORKING DRAFT (2026-06-15). TIME is one axis with two faces: SCHEDULE (plan-forward, over the work graph) and DYNAMICS (behavior over time, over the loop). Included as an axis (the insight), not as engines — see v1 scope below.

The two faces

FaceOverKey insightExample
SCHEDULEthe work graph (atoms + handoff edges + durations)the critical path is the deadline floor; effort off it changes nothingspeed a slack atom → deadline unmoved
DYNAMICSthe loop (stocks + delayed feedback)locally-rational + delays → oscillation; the pathology is in the trajectory, not any atomfan out to clear backlog → burst-finish → idle → repeat
Schedule's finite-resource contention is the Resource axis on the clock (it extends the critical path). Dynamics' guardrail is canon pitfalls #2 / #12: don't "tamper" against normal variance.

v1 product surface (honest scope)

TIME ships as diagnostics, not engines. No scheduler, no system-dynamics simulator in v1.

Facev1 surface
SCHEDULErecord-derived annotations — "this chain is the critical path", "this atom has slack" — and planning hints, not a solver that sequences for you
DYNAMICSrecord-derived warnings — "backlog is oscillating", "this stock is draining" — surfaced as review prompts, not a forward simulation
The loop models the work graph + its evolution — NOT on-time or stability guarantees. A fully-verified atom set can still miss a deadline (schedule) or oscillate (dynamics); TIME makes those visible as diagnostics so an operator can act. The solver and the simulator are the implementation layer — deferred.
