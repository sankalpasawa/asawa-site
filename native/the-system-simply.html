What it is, in one paragraph

Native is a feedback loop for work: it turns an intent into done work, writes down what happened, and reads its own record to do better next time. The record is the system's model of itself (the Good Regulator Theorem: to regulate something well you must model it — the record is that model). Everything below is detail inside that one loop.

1 · The two primitives

Build these two well; everything else is them, composed — these are the two product primitives this narrative foregrounds; canon carries 10 typed primitives in total (see §8).

2 · The flow (the loop)

An intent enters → SHAPE turns it into a plan of atoms → they RUN and leave a RECORD → GROW reads the record and improves things → the improvement reshapes the next run. Authority wraps every step.

3 · The axes — one primitive, sorted into families

An axis is a dimension you read a piece of work against. Axes are not a fixed list — they are minted on demand from one primitive (value ↔ axis, ADR-027). What is fixed is the primitive + the families (the slots an axis falls into), sorted by one read-test.

Families = closed skeleton; axes = open, minted flesh. Solid arrows = the original three families (unit-internal); dashed = the three this session surfaced (between-atoms, stock, time).

The three EDGE kinds

Shared-resource edges + Scheduling contention are the same thing — the S2 (coordination) / S3 (resource) rungs of the maturation ladder. TIME dynamics guardrail (canon pitfalls #2 / #12): respond to out-of-control signals only — don't "tamper" against normal variance.

5 · The axes are open — minting vs reworking

Mirrors the two primitives: the Work-Atom is the one doing primitive (everything is it, composed); value↔axis is the one thinking primitive (every axis is it, minted). Users mint axes; only the core author reworks the families.

7 · Honest scope

  • Productization, not implementation. Schemas, thresholds, solvers, simulators are the implementation layer — deferred.
  • TIME is included as axes (the insight), not engines. No scheduler, no system-dynamics simulator in v1 — the critical-path idea and the oscillation warning, yes.
  • The loop models the work graph + its evolution — NOT on-time or stability guarantees. A fully-verified atom set can still miss a deadline or oscillate.

8 · Corrections kept honest

  • Native does NOT lack composition/coordination. Canon already has 5 composition laws + the VSM metasystem; the website pages just under-surface it — this doc surfaces it.
  • Native has more than one canon primitive. Work-Atom is the hero of this narrative; canon lists 10 typed primitives (Domain, Charter, Workflow, Step, Trigger, ExecutionResult, EngineEvent, Tenant, DecisionProvenance, Approval).

In this section — the deep dives

  • Axis-productization — how any axis (incl. a user-minted one) becomes product: P1–P5 + a new-axis worked example + the retention gate.
  • Axes + families — the open set, the 6 families, the sorting test.
  • The open-axis rule — mint on the fly / the core author reworks the families.
  • Edges — the 3 typed links (handoff · shared-resource · must-cohere).
  • Resource — the finite stock work spends + allocation.
  • Time — schedule + dynamics (v1 = diagnostics, not engines).
Two refinements (from the codex two-deep review): (1) the loop is RECORD → EVIDENCE → GROW — the record becomes evidence the moment it is read across runs (the read-model form before GROW). (2) verify's "accept" half is owned by a counter-party — a human (Approval), another tenant, or a parent workflow — gated by Authority; it is not the executor grading its own homework.
